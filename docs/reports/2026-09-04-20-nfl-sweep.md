# The NFL sweep — 103 seasons, and three things the declarations got wrong

**2026-09-04. Report 20.** StatsCrew NFL, all seasons. Summary at
`dataset/build-reports/sweep-nfl.json`, full field census at
`field-census.txt` / `field-census.json`.

## Result

    seasons        103        1922-2024, none missing
    player-rows    117,300
    teams          18 (1922) -> 32 (2002 on), the 1970 merger visible at 16 -> 26
    cached pages   2,200+     one fetch each, ~1s apart, never re-fetched

## First: I made the declarations load-bearing, because they weren't

The brief asked me to check the ingest **reads** the declarations rather than
happening to work on 1950. **It did not.** `JERSEY_USABLE` and `GP_LEAGUE_RATE`
were computed at the top of `ingest_season.py` and **never referenced** — the
ingest handled columns by presence on the page, which is behaviourally right and
consulted nothing.

Now every run measures actual fill per field and compares it against what the
declaration predicts, flagging any gap over 10 points. **That check is what found
everything below.**

## THE FINDING: column presence is a PER-PAGE property

Report 06 declared `jersey` as `kind: per_era` from four teams per season. The
full census contradicts it:

| season | teams with a jersey column | missing on |
|---|---|---|
| 1922 | 15/18 | CHC, EVA, RI |
| 1923 | 17/20 | CHC, CL2, SLA |
| 1924 | 15/18 | CHC, COL, DUL |
| 1925 | 17/20 | DUL, HAM, MIL |
| 1929 | 11/12 | ORA |
| 1931 | 9/10 | FYJ |
| 1943 | 8/9 | PIT |
| **2020** | **31/32** | **LVR** |

**Eight seasons contain teams that lack the column while their contemporaries
carry it — and it is not an early-era phenomenon.** Las Vegas lacks it in 2020.

**And no NFL season lacks it entirely.** `column_absent_entirely` is true of
APFA 1920 alone, not of the pre-1935 era.

**So there is a third kind, alongside per-era and per-league: per-page.** The
declaration's `kind` field now reads `per_era_AND_per_page`, with the eight
seasons and their teams listed. This is report 06's per-league finding one level
finer — **a source's coverage varies by the individual document, not only by the
population it belongs to**, and a four-team sample cannot see it by construction.

## Second finding: two seasons the declaration got wrong

**`games_started` reads 89.8% in 2013 and 89.2% in 2014**, against a declared
100% for the NFL. Report 06 sampled 1960, 1965, 1970, 1980, 1990, 2000, 2010 and
2020 — **it skipped both.** Two consecutive seasons, ~10 points low, recorded as
exceptions on the declaration.

## Third: a bug in my own fetcher that poisoned 29 seasons

The first sweep reported **29 consecutive failures from 1996**, DNS errors. On
retry every one of them returned **"0 teams"** and failed the match-rate gate.

**The cause was mine.** `_get` opened the cache file *before* fetching:

```python
open(p, "wb").write(urllib.request.urlopen(req).read())   # WRONG
```

`open(...,"wb")` creates the file, *then* `urlopen` raises. **The DNS outage left
29 zero-byte files, and the cache served them forever as valid empty pages.**

**This is the archive.org trap the roster-project handoff records** — *"a 302
without `-L` writes a zero-byte file that reads exactly like a book with no
text"* — arriving from a completely different cause. Same shape: a failed fetch
that is indistinguishable from an empty document.

Fixed: **fetch first, refuse an empty response, write to a temp file, rename
atomically**, and delete any zero-byte entry found on read.

**The fix proved itself within the hour.** The 2018 run hit a read timeout — and
left **zero** zero-byte files behind, where the old code would have poisoned that
season too. `find -size 0` across 2,200 cached pages returns nothing.

> **A cache must not be able to store a failure.**

## What the census gives that report 06 could not

Report 06 measured 4 teams per league-year; this is **every team of every
season** — 117,300 player-rows against roughly 4,600. The declaration's
`measured` values are now census figures rather than a sample, and the two
disagree in the ways above.

**Fill is otherwise remarkably stable.** Birth date, college, GP and GS run at or
near 100% across the whole span; the 1922–1934 jersey rates (76.6% rising to
94.8%) track report 06's sampled figures closely, which is the sample being
validated rather than overturned.

## Rate and caching

One request per second or slower, every page cached on first fetch, **never
pulled twice** — a re-run of an already-swept season touches no network at all.
That is what made the 2018 retry and the three corrective passes cheap.
