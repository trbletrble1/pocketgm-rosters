## The court cases — seven of them, and the provenance is unimprovable

**These are findings of fact in federal opinions.** No attribution chain, no
interested party reporting, no OCR of a scan. This is the strongest-provenance
salary material the project has ever held, and there are seven documents of it.

### 1. Smith v. Pro-Football — verified, and the relay was exactly right

    James "Yazoo" Smith, Washington, 1968
      signing bonus     $23,000
      roster bonus      $ 5,000   ("an additional $5,000 if he made the team")
      salary            $22,000
      option year       $19,800
      TOTAL             $69,800

    Pat Fischer, Washington, 1968-69 (two-year, no-cut)
      salary            $30,000 per year
      bonus             $48,000 over the two years

**Both cautions in the brief are correct and confirmed on the page.** The
**$162,000** is the court's estimate of free-market value — *"the Court adds the
proportionate share ($54,000) for the extra year, for a total three-year figure of
$162,000"* — **not a salary**, and it is one row away from $150,000, which is the
*agent's deposition estimate*, also not a salary. Three non-salary figures sit
among the real ones.

**The option-year rule is stated verbatim** in footnote 8: *"the standard
option-year pay of 90% of the prior year's salary"*. And it **self-checks**:
$19,800 is exactly 90% of $22,000. That is a structural fact about the reserve
system, worth holding separately from any contract.

### 2. Alabama Football v. Greenwood — a contract transcribed from the instrument

The opinion quotes the typed terms out of the WFL standard form:

    "$25,000.00 Bonus due upon signing
     $25,000.00 due Sept. 1, 1974
     April 1, 1975 $25,000.00 Bal due on Bonus
     1975 $90,000.00   1976 $100,000.00   1977 $110,000.00"

**L.C. Greenwood, Birmingham Americans — $375,000 over three years.** Also **Ken
Stabler** at $70,000 for 1974 from the same club, and the club's own finances:
liabilities $2.2M against assets $1.7M, a $500,000 WFL entry fee, and a
$151,635.97 default.

### 3. Bengals v. Bergey — the same man, the same year, two leagues

    Bill Bergey, 1974
      Cincinnati Bengals (NFL)        $ 38,750
      Virginia Ambassadors (WFL)      $125,000 per year, no-cut,
                                      plus a $150,000 bonus ($40,000 at signing)
      Bengals counter-offer           ~$400,000 package over five years

**A starting NFL linebacker on $38,750 while the WFL offered $125,000.** No other
source in this project puts one player's two markets side by side in the same
season, from a neutral finder of fact.

### 4-7. The others

**Beathard** — Pete Beathard $12,000 bonus + $70,000 for 1975 (Chicago Winds,
WFL), paid in 18 instalments; Lawrence Jameson $10,500 + $25,000; both backed by
letters of credit, and both unpaid — Beathard claiming $50,555.55, Jameson
$19,440.00. **A contract and its default in one document.**

**Kapp** — four contracts across three leagues: Houston 1967 ($10,000 report
bonus, $100,000/yr for two years, voided by Rozelle); $50,000 paid to his Canadian
club for release; Minnesota 1967–69 total $300,000; New England 1970–72 total
$600,000, of which **$154,000 actually paid**.

**Rivers** — the contract clause verbatim: *"the amount of $65,000.00"* per season,
plus board and lodging.

**Hayes** — two alternative 1974 Rams contracts signed the same day: $8,000
signing bonus with $22,000 annual, or $26,000 annual with different bonus
provisions. **A rare view of what a club offered as alternatives.**

## The newspapers — and one is the per-position table

### UD, 14 January 1985 — 1984 salaries by position, 52 named players

Page 10 reprints a **Dallas Morning News** table across ten position blocks:
QUARTERBACKS, RUNNING BACKS, WIDE RECEIVERS, TIGHT ENDS, OFFENSIVE LINE, DEFENSIVE
LINE, LINEBACKERS, DEFENSIVE BACKS, PLACE KICKERS, PUNTERS.

Warren Moon $1.1M, Elway $900,000, Montana $858,333, Dickey $850,000, Billy Sims
$800,000, Payton $785,000 … down to punters at $145,000–$160,000.

**And it states its methodology, which is the part that matters:**

> *Figures reflect 1984 base salary, reporting and roster bonuses and signing
> bonus **pro-rated over the length of the contract**. For example: Detroit's Billy
> Sims signed a 5-year contract with a $1 million bonus. His 1984 salary was
> $600,000; his pro-rated signing bonus is $200,000 a year. In the listing, his
> salary is listed at $800,000.*

**That is the Management Council convention Newman's Appendix C describes** —
the one that revised §8.4 — here applied, explained, and worked through an
example. It also says 1984 ran *"roughly a 25 percent increase over 1983"*, which
matches Newman's Appendix C exactly.

### Pampa News, 18 October 1984 — and it names a new link in the chain

An AP story quoting the Dallas Morning News, *"quoting figures supplied by the
teams to the NFL Players Association."*

**That is the clubs as the origin**, which no other source in this project has
stated. The chain is long — Pampa ← AP ← DMN ← NFLPA ← **the clubs** — but the
far end is named and it is the League side, not the union's own survey.

Danny White **$550,000** (highest-paid Cowboy, 1984); Randy White **$450,000** base
plus a **$600,000** signing bonus which *"if prorated over the length of his
contract, would add [$120,000] a year … putting him at $570,000"*.

**The OCR reads `1120,000` and the arithmetic corrects it**: $600,000 over five
years is $120,000, and $450,000 + $120,000 = $570,000. **And it cross-checks the UD
table**, which lists Randy White at exactly $570,000. Two documents, one dataset —
**one lineage, not two witnesses.**

### The two feature articles — both NFLPA survey lineage

**Cayman Compass, 11 April 1986** (Clark Judge): the 1985 top-50 *"according to
surveys compiled by the NFL Players Association"*, explicitly **base salary only,
"contracts stripped of bonuses or incentives"** — the opposite convention to the
DMN table, and the difference must travel with the numbers. Montana $900,000
(1985), $950,000 (1986), $1M (1987), $1.2M (1989), all guaranteed and none
deferred.

**LA Times / AP, 25 January 1988**: 1987 figures *"prepared by the NFL Players
Assn. and published in the Boston Sunday Herald"* — three removes. Dickerson $1.4M
a year plus a $341,000 signing bonus; **league average $211,506 across 1,683
players, up from $202,960 in 1986.** Those two averages do not continue Newman's
series (which ends 1985 at $193,300) and appear to be a later, differently-drawn
survey.

## Toni Fritsch, 1984 — the thing every other source excludes

A blog post analysing a photographed USFL contract (the image is item 13, a 300px
thumbnail — too small to read independently).

    Houston Gamblers, 1 Dec 1983 - 30 Nov 1984
      salary                                    $35,000
      60% FG on 18+ attempts                    $ 5,000
      USFL Special Teams Player of the Year      $ 2,500
      first-team All-USFL (AP/PFW/PFWA/NEA)      $10,000
      lead league in kicking points              $ 8,000
      100% of PATs                               $ 2,000

**Every aggregate source in this project explicitly excludes performance
bonuses** — the hearing, the primer, the DMN table all say so. **This document is
the thing they exclude**, itemised, for one man.

Caution: the author hedges — *"If my research is correct, Fritsch added $23,000 in
bonuses to his $35,000 salary"* — and the outcomes (which bonuses were actually
earned) are the blogger's reconstruction, not the contract. **The clause list is
the contract; the payout is analysis.**

## What this changes

**The pre-1990 contract gap is no longer a gap.** Seven federal opinions carry
complete contracts with components broken out — signing bonus, roster bonus,
salary, option year — for 1967 through 1975, at **zero removes**. That is better
provenance than everything reported in reports 01, 02, 05, 07 and 11 combined.

**Three conventions are now visible and must never be pooled:**

| convention | source | includes |
|---|---|---|
| base salary only | Cayman Compass / NFLPA survey | no bonuses, no incentives |
| base + prorated bonus | DMN table, UD and Pampa | signing bonus spread over contract length |
| total compensation | the court cases | every component, itemised separately |

**Two cautions carried forward.** Smith's $162,000 and $150,000 are court and agent
*estimates*, not salaries. And UD and Pampa are **one lineage** — the same Dallas
Morning News dataset — confirmed by Randy White reading $570,000 in both.

## Recommended order, if extraction is approved

1. **The seven court cases first.** Zero removes, itemised components, and the
   option-year rule as a structural claim in its own right.
2. **The UD position table**, with its methodology note attached to every figure.
3. **Fritsch**, as the incentive schedule the aggregates exclude.
4. The three survey-lineage articles last — they are the weakest provenance here
   and partly duplicate each other.
