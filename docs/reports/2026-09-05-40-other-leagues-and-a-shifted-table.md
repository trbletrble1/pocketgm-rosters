## The defect: a two-row header shifting every value

You said the other leagues would have vocabulary the NFL doesn't. Chasing one of
those — `FGS`, which I had reported as a one-season NFL anomaly — found something
worse.

**`FGS` is not an NFL anomaly at all. It is CFL-only, 97.5% filled: *field goal
singles*, the rouge.** So is `PS`, punt singles. I had censused a cache that was
being written underneath me: at the time, the only CFL stats page on disk was
`CFLBC_1979`, fetched two hours earlier during a shape probe. One CFL page read as
an NFL one-year experiment.

*A new shape of an old error: not sample-versus-census, but **censusing a moving
target**.*

Chasing why `Single` read 100% in the NFL then found the real defect. **The
scoring table has a two-row header:**

```html
<tr> <th colspan=8>Touchdowns</th> <th colspan=5>Other</th> <th>Total</th> </tr>
<tr> <th>Player</th> <th>Rush</th> <th>Rec</th> ... <th>Points</th> </tr>
```

Flattening both gave **19 "columns" for a 15-column table**, and chunking shifted
every value:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| Billy Grimes, GB 1950 | `Touchdowns=5, Other=1, Total=2, Player=0` | `Rush=5, Rec=1, Punt=2, Points=48` |

The fix takes the **last** `<tr>` of the `<thead>`. Grimes led the NFL in punt
return touchdowns in 1950, so the corrected row is checkable and checks out. And
`2Pt` and `Saf` now come back **blank** for 1950 rather than carrying another
column's numbers — correct, since neither existed.

**Effect on the 1950 ingest:** rows **1,048 → 1,202**, cells **5,857 → 7,663**,
and **rows refused for having no slug: 101 → 0**, because the Player column was
being read out of position.

*This is the third parser defect in this corpus, and all three were caught by a
number that could not be true: a column filled 84% before the measure existed,
`Single` populated in a league without singles, `Sacked` filled in a league that
folded twenty years before sacks were recorded.*

## And a fourth: identity by an insufficient key

`Sacked` came back **100% filled in the AAFC** — a league that folded in 1949,
against a column the source carries only from 1969.

My league attribution mapped **team → league**. Cleveland and San Francisco played
in the AAFC *and then* the NFL, so whichever store loaded last won. Keyed on
`(team, year)` the AAFC disappears from `Sacked` and from `Tackle`, as it must.

**The same failure as `p_000190` meaning three men** — a key that does not
determine what it is used to determine.

## The granularity, arrived at by measurement

You predicted the per-league-season lesson would apply. It did, in three steps,
each one measured by deviations rather than chosen:

| declaration keyed on | deviations, NFL 1950 |
|---|---|
| pooled decade | **9** |
| per league | **4** |
| **per league × decade** | **0** |

`Tackle` is the clearest: the NFL's all-time figure is 52.9%, and 1950's is 0%.
A per-league declaration is still wrong for every early season.

**Third time this lesson has arrived** — `per_era`, then `per_league_season` on
the rosters, now league × era on the columns.

The declaration is generated by `src/declare_stat_columns.py` and carries
`fill_by_league_decade` for every column.

## What the leagues actually record differently

| column | who carries it |
|---|---|
| **`FGS`** field goal singles | **CFL only**, 97.5% |
| `2Pt` | USFL2 100%, WFL 100%, USFL 99%, XFL 37%, CFL 19%, NFL 8%, AFL 7% |
| `Tackle` | UFL2/USFL2 100%, UFL 97%, XFL 94%, AAF 86%, CFL 82%, NFL 53% |
| `Sacked` | every league from 1969; **AAFC and APFA carry it not at all** |

The `2Pt` ordering is history: the WFL's action point from 1974, the USFL from
1983, the AFL from 1960, the NFL only from 1994.

**One caution recorded: `Single` is 100% filled in every league, including leagues
with no such scoring play.** The source uses one schema throughout and writes `0`
rather than leaving the cell empty. **Fill is not applicability** — a column can be
fully populated and semantically empty, and no fill-based check can tell.
