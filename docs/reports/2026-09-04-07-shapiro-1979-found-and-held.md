## Verified verbatim

Every figure relayed to this session was **correct**. Worth stating, because the
Dean relay carried a dating error and the Cowboys relay understated the chain by
one remove.

**League aggregate.** Average salary rose **13.2%** from **$55,000** (1977) to
**$62,585** (1978), *"computed by the NFL's Management Council"*. Exclusions on
the page: *"did not include performance (incentive) clauses or playoff money."*

**Positional increases 1977→1978 — deltas, not levels.** QB +$13,000, OL +$8,000,
LB +$8,000, DL +$7,000, DB +$7,000, K +$7,000, WR +$6,000, RB +$2,000.

**Nineteen named players**, filling the positions every other source misses —
offensive line, linebacker, defensive back, kicker: Simpson $733,358, Payton
$431,500, Tarkenton $360,000, Pastorini $358,333, Stabler $342,000, Riggins
$300,000, Jackson $237,657, Kilmer $230,000, Bergey $213,333, Campbell $199,666,
Greene $174,000, Hannah $173,966, Blount $141,870, Hanburger $125,000, Curtis
$115,000, Talbert $105,000, McDole $100,000, Houston $96,000, Yepremian $90,000.

## The structural description, verbatim

> *The Management Council also broke down salaries from first-year players to
> 20-year players, by position, listing the highest salary paid, the lowest salary
> paid, the average and the median salary.*

And the released/withheld split, also verbatim: the by-position average and median
figures were *"made public recently"* — dating that release to **early 1979** —
while the service-year breakdown was *"not made public"*.

## The finding neither of us had: the withheld table is nearly individual

Shapiro describes **how** he deduced the named players, and the method reveals the
table's information content.

> *In the running back category, for example, were only three 10-year veterans
> still active in 1978 — Simpson, Larry Csonka … and Rocky Bleier…*

> *There were only two 14th-year linebackers in the NFL in 1978 and both were
> Redskins. Chris Hanburger earned $125,000, with Mike Curtis at $115,000.*

**At high service years the cells hold one, two or three men.** So the withheld
half of the document is not merely aggregate — for veterans it is close to a
per-player salary list, recoverable by anyone who can enumerate the league's
players by position and service year.

**That raises the debt's value considerably.** The owed table was logged as a
per-position, per-experience *structure*. It is that at low service years and
effectively an individual salary record at high ones. It is also the reason
Shapiro could publish nineteen names from a document that named nobody.

## Three provenance tiers, marked by the author

Per §3.6 each gets its own attribution and they must not be flattened:

1. **Stated as fact** — most figures.
2. **Explicitly hedged** — Blount and Talbert, both *"is believed to have
   earned"*.
3. **Declared derivation** — *"Using those figures (which were not made public),
   previously published salary estimates and interviews with several highly placed
   NFL sources, it is possible to deduce…"*

Tier 3 is **`source_derived` announced by the source**, which is better than the
2002 primer's silence and better than most of what this project holds. A source
that tells you which of its numbers are deduced is doing the work §3.3 exists to
force.

## Garvey, and what it is worth

> *Ed Garvey, executive director of the NFL players Association, said he thought
> the figures released by the Management Council "on the whole are accurate. I
> have no reason to doubt them."*

**The NFLPA's executive director declining to dispute the League's figures** — a
year before the hearing in which the same two organisations disagree by 35% on the
1979 average.

It is **not** a League-original source and does not discharge the debt. But a
concession by the opposing party is evidence of a different and stronger kind than
either party's own assertion, and it bears directly on §8.4. Worth weighing when
that contest is next examined: the NFLPA disputed the League's 1979 figure while
having accepted its 1978 one.
