## Page two, and it settles three questions

I had been reading first pages. The Askea lot's page 2 is the most informative
document in the batch.

**¶5 RELEASE** names the vehicle: the player releases the WFL, its governors past
and present, the 1974 club and its owners, the WFL Player's Association, and **the
WFL Player's Compensation Trust and its Trustees.**

**¶6, the 1975 contract promise, is struck through** — with *"see back page"*
written beside it. The clause promised:

> *"Club shall tender to Player on or before May 30, 1975, a Standard 1975 WFL
> Player's Contract which shall provide for a minimum of **$500.00 per regularly
> scheduled season game** for which Player is on 'active' status"*

**The whole numbered clause was deleted and replaced.** Third instance of *a
deletion is data* — after Pearce's amended percentages and Franco's struck release
proviso — and the first where an entire clause goes.

**And the minimum is not uniform.** Askea's reads **$500.00**; Billingsley's
bleed-through reads **$1,000.00**. Two players, two figures — which is precisely
why each document has to be read rather than the form assumed.

**¶7 answers the counterpart question, and the document got there first:**

> *"contains their entire understanding **in four copies**"*

Ron Billingsley appears in two lots at $31,500 with identical wording. I had
concluded by comparison that those were two counterparts rather than two debts.
**The instrument says four copies exist.** The document confirmed the inference —
and would have prevented the error had I read page 2 before page 1.

Governed by the laws of the State of **Oregon**.

## What the batch can and cannot answer

**Can:** what each man was owed, on what schedule, by whom — and in Billingsley's
version the **league is a co-obligor** where in Silas's it is not.

**Cannot:** what any of them was *contracted* to earn. **Every document in the
batch is the addendum**, and the 1974 contract it extinguishes is not among them.
The hoped-for contract/settlement pair did not materialise — Billingsley's second
document is a counterpart, not the other half.

So the batch gives **debts without salaries**. Which is still a thing nothing else
in the archive holds.
