# 42 — Fifty bios, and what they cannot say

2026-09-05. A prototype, not a build. **Fifty paragraphs, every sentence from a
claim.** Full set: `dataset/build-reports/bios-sample-50.txt`.

---

## First: the `Single` caution, promoted and enforced

Moved out of a report and into `statscrew.json` as **`stat_columns.applicability`**,
with the general form stated:

> **A source that writes zero where a measure does not apply is
> indistinguishable, by fill, from one that measured zero.**

**And all-zero cannot settle it either.** `Single` is all-zero across 29,028 NFL
values because the rouge is Canadian — but `MFG`, missed field goal return
touchdowns, is all-zero across *the same* 29,028 values, and that play is legal.
It simply never happened. **Inapplicable and never-occurred are indistinguishable
in the data**, so applicability is *declared*, not inferred: `Single` and `FGS`
ruled CFL-only, `MFG` ruled applicable everywhere (its zeros are real
measurements), and six columns left explicitly **unresolved** and flagged on every
claim.

Enforced at the write. `gate_applicability` failed on the shipped stores — 179
`Single` claims in NFL 1950 alone, plus WLAF, USFL2, UFL, APFA — and passes after
a full re-ingest. **54,156 claims checked, 0 written where the measure does not
apply.**

*And it caught me committing the precedent I had just written down:* I nested
`applicability` **inside** `ZERO_IS_NOT_ABSENCE`, a block marked
documentation-only, so nothing read it and the guard silently passed everything.
**Fourth instance of "a declaration in the wrong place is not a declaration",
minutes after writing the rule.** Found because NFL 1950 refused zero `Single`
values when it should have refused 179.

## What is thin — the point of the exercise

**1. No club is named. Anywhere.** *0 club-name claims in the archive.* Every bio
says "1924 with MI1", "with CLR", "with HOO". A reader cannot know these are the
Kenosha Maroons, the Cleveland Rams, the Houston Oilers. **This is the single
biggest legibility gap and the cheapest to close.**

**2. Position codes are raw and unmapped.** **2,298 distinct codes**, no mapping
declared anywhere. `ROE-RDE-LO` is right — era-native, as designed — but no reader
knows it means a right end who played both ways. The design always said
translation happens at export, per era, and *the mapping is itself data*. **That
data does not exist yet.**

**3. Nothing knows which statistic matters for which position.** Junior Seau first
came out with "238 yards" — his interception return yards — because my generator
hardcoded yards. **His 1,686 tackles were in the archive the whole time.** The fix
was to surface the largest counting total and *name the column*; the real fix is a
declared position→salient-statistic mapping, which is the same missing data as (2).

**4. No superlatives.** As you predicted, the bios reach for them and cannot get
there. Nothing can say *led the league*, *most in a game*, *first to*. Season
statistics are loaded, so **league leaders are computable** — a max over
`(league, season, column)`. Records and firsts are not: they need the
records-and-leaders sections that are **29% of the guides' statistical headings**,
characterised in report 38 and set aside. *Noted, not acted on.*

**5. Coaches read as players.** "Mike McCarthy … played 18 seasons from 2006 to
2024." He never played. A coaching stint and a playing stint are the same `stint`
subject, distinguished only by a `role_title` claim the sentence-builder checks
too late.

**6. Careers end without ending.** 403 death dates in 40,745 people, and no
retirement, release or final-game claim. Every bio simply stops.

**7. 23% have no statistics at all**, concentrated in the early era and among
linemen — for whom nothing was recorded rather than nothing having happened.

---

## What I would fix first

**Club names.** One claim per club-season, from a source that states them —
PFA's page titles carry them ("1946 Hamilton Tigers (IRFU)"), already on disk for
418 pages. It turns every bio from a code into a sentence, and nothing else on
this list is that cheap.

Then the position map, which unblocks both (2) and (3).
