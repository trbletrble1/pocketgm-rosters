## Two documents are a different instrument entirely

### 1938 Ed Franco — not the NFL, and not a season salary

> "The **Union City Football Club, Inc.** … The Club is a member of **The American
> Professional Football Association**"
> "at the rate of **$100. per game**. As to games scheduled but not played the
> player shall receive no compensation … other than actual expenses"

And handwritten onto the form:

> **"but said player has option of said guarantee or 3% of the gross receipts of
> all home games"**

**A player's election between a per-game guarantee and a share of the gate.**
Nothing in the archive has that shape. Paid *"after each game"*. And paragraph 4's
release proviso is **struck through** — the deletion is itself data, and a
transcription reading only the printed text would record the opposite of what was
agreed.

**A league-name collision, the `AFL3` shape again:** this "American Professional
Football Association" is a 1930s minor league. Our `APFA` code means the 1920–21
league that became the NFL. Recorded as a trap.

### The Portland Storm batch is not thirteen contracts

You described it as thirteen contracts from one club in one 1974 season. Read:

> **ADDENDUM TO 1974 PLAYER'S CONTRACT** — between the player, *"A Club in the
> **1975** World Football League"*, and the WFL as a non-profit corporation.
>
> *"such 1974 WFL Club is not a necessary party since it **failed to fulfil its
> basic contractual obligation to pay player for services rendered** … hereby
> **extinguish** the 1974 Standard WFL Player's Contract"*
>
> *"Club will pay to Player a total sum of **$15,749.95** … **(which is
> undisputedly owed to Player)**"*

**These are debt settlements.** $15,749.95 is what the Storm **did not pay** Sam
Silas. Repayment: 15% on completion of the *WFL Reorganization*, the balance in
semi-annual installments at **6% simple annual interest**, funded from the **WFL
Contingency Fund** — into which clubs deposit 1% of net ticket receipts, 1% of WFL
properties income, and 5% of net national television revenue from 1976.

**So the player's recovery depends on the league's revenues, not his club's
promise — and whether he was ever paid is not in the document.**

Wallace Williams carries the identical header, so the batch is settlements.
Declared as `unpaid_wages_settlement`, with the rule that it may never pool with
salary. **Filed as salary these thirteen figures would have recorded the exact
opposite of what happened** — and they are more interesting as what they are: the
WFL's collapse, documented player by player.
