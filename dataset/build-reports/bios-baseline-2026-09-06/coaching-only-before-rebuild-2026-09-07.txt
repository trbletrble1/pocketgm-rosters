FIFTEEN MEN WHO ONLY EVER COACHED

1. Jack Keogh spent one season as Head Coach of the Hartford Blues in 1926. He died on February 13, 1955.

2. Alfred McGall spent one season as Head Coach of the Newark Tornadoes in 1930. He died on September 8, 1941.

3. Bill Edwards spent two seasons as Head Coach of the Detroit Lions from 1941 to 1942. He died on June 12, 1987.

4. Jimmy Phelan coached in the AAFC and the NFL, most of it as Head Coach of the Los Angeles Dons from 1948 to 1949. In all he coached three seasons across two clubs, 1948 to 1952. He held the same job at the Dallas Texans. He was away from the game from 1950 to 1951. He died on November 14, 1974.

5. Lisle Blackbourn was Head Coach of the Green Bay Packers from 1954 to 1957. He died on June 14, 1983.

6. Over twenty-seven seasons, 1969 to 1997, Marv Levy was Head Coach of the Buffalo Bills from 1986 to 1997. He coached for seven clubs in all. He held the same job at the Montreal Alouettes, the Kansas City Chiefs and the Chicago Blitz. He was in and out of the game, missing 1983 and 1985. He had been an assistant for four seasons before that.

7. Chuck Studley coached twenty-three seasons, 1969 to 1991, and became Head Coach of the Houston Oilers in 1983. He coached for four clubs in all. He spent another twenty-two seasons as an assistant.

8. Charlie Waller's coaching crossed leagues — the AFL and the NFL — and its longest stretch was as Head Coach of the San Diego Chargers from 1969 to 1970. He died on September 5, 2009.

9. John McVay's coaching crossed leagues — the WFL and the NFL — and its longest stretch was as Head Coach of the New York Giants from 1976 to 1978. In all he coached five seasons across two clubs, 1974 to 1978. He held the same job at the Memphis Southmen.

10. Ron Erhardt spent four seasons as Head Coach of the New England Patriots from 1978 to 1981. He died on March 21, 2012.

11. Over twenty-two seasons, 1983 to 2008, Don Matthews was Head Coach of the Montreal Alouettes from 2002 to 2006. He coached for seven clubs in all. He was a head coach at eight other clubs, among them the BC Lions, the Toronto Argonauts and the Orlando Thunder. He was in and out of the game, missing 1988–89, 2001 and 2007.

12. Urban Bowman became Head Coach of the Hamilton Tiger-Cats in 1997. He coached two seasons in all, for two clubs, from 1992 to 1997. He held the same job at the Winnipeg Blue Bombers. He was away from the game from 1993 to 1996.

13. Romeo Crennel was Head Coach of the Cleveland Browns from 2005 to 2008. He coached seven seasons in all, for three clubs, from 2005 to 2020. He held the same job at the Kansas City Chiefs and the Houston Texans. He was in and out of the game, missing 2009–10 and 2013–19.

14. Craig Dickenson was Head Coach of the Saskatchewan Roughriders from 2021 to 2023. He coached four seasons in all, from 2019 to 2023. He did not coach in 2020.

15. Curtis Johnson coached in the USFL and the UFL, most of it as Head Coach of the Houston Roughnecks in 2024. He coached two seasons in all, for two clubs, from 2023 to 2024. He held the same job at the Houston Gamblers.

16. In 1953, playing for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers, Bud Grant caught 68 passes for 922 yards — only one man in the CFL caught more that season. He played six seasons: for the Philadelphia Eagles from 1951 to 1952 and the Winnipeg Blue Bombers from 1953 to 1956. He was later Head Coach of the Minnesota Vikings from 1967 to 1983. He was a head coach at two other clubs too. He died on March 11, 2023.
