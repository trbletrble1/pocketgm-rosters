8. One game was the whole of Mekhi Brown's career: the Tampa Bay Bandits, 2022, in the USFL.

9. Leon Winternheimer played two seasons for the Evansville Crimson Giants of the APFA, from 1921 to 1922.

10. John Fahay played in the NFL with the Milwaukee Badgers (1925), the Racine Tornadoes (1926) and the Minneapolis Red Jackets (1929) and in the AFL with the Chicago Bulls (1926). He was out of the game from 1927 to 1928. He died on January 8, 1980, in Fort Lauderdale, FL.

11. Al McIntosh spent two seasons with the Providence Steam Roller, of the NFL, from 1925 to 1926.

12. Lou Merillat spent a single season, 1925, with the Canton Bulldogs. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

13. Stan Keck played two professional games, both for the Cleveland Indians in 1923. He died on January 20, 1951, in Pittsburgh, PA.

14. Mal Bross moved between leagues: the AFL with the Wilson's Wildcats (1926); the NFL with the Green Bay Packers (1927). He died on February 8, 1989, in Seattle, WA.

15. Bert Kuczynski played for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and did not play again until 1946, when he joined the Philadelphia Eagles. He played two seasons: for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1946. He was a United States Navy veteran.

16. Nick Kerasiotis's last game before the war came in 1942 with the Chicago Bears; his next, in 1945, was with the same club. He served in the United States Navy.

17. Dixie Stokes was with the Detroit Lions in 1939, then out of football until 1943 and the Chicago Cardinals. His four seasons were with the Detroit Lions from 1937 to 1939 and the Chicago Cardinals in 1943.

18. Joe Gibson was with the Cleveland Rams in 1944, then out of football until 1946 and the Brooklyn Dodgers. His five seasons were with the Cleveland Rams in 1942 and 1944, the Washington Redskins in 1943 and the Brooklyn Dodgers from 1946 to 1947. He was a United States Navy veteran.

19. Hank Rockwell was with the Cleveland Rams in 1942, then out of football until 1946 and the Los Angeles Dons. He played five seasons: for the Cleveland Rams from 1940 to 1942 and the Los Angeles Dons in 1946 and 1948. He did not play in 1947. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

20. Al Wilson played fifteen seasons, every one of them for the BC Lions, from 1972 to 1986.

21. Cliff Livingston's career ran twelve seasons, 1954 to 1965, beginning with the New York Giants. From there he went to the Minnesota Vikings in 1962 and the Los Angeles Rams from 1963 to 1965. Over five seasons he intercepted 8 passes.

22. Brian Robison played eleven seasons, every one of them for the Minnesota Vikings, from 2007 to 2017. Over eleven seasons he made 244 tackles.

23. From 1965 to 1976, twelve seasons, Al Ford played for nobody but the Saskatchewan Roughriders. In all he caught 261 passes over twelve seasons.

24. Robin Cole's career ran twelve seasons, 1977 to 1988, beginning with the Pittsburgh Steelers. He moved on to the New York Jets in 1988. In all he intercepted 5 passes over five seasons.

25. In 1955, playing for the Toronto Argonauts, Tom Dublinski completed 225 passes for 3,561 yards — only two men in the CFL completed more that season. Over ten seasons he played for five clubs: the Detroit Lions, the Toronto Argonauts, the New York Giants, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats and the Denver Broncos. He did not play in 1956.

26. Henry Slay moved between leagues: the NFL with the Philadelphia Eagles (1998); the XFL with the New York/New Jersey Hitmen (2001). He was out of the game from 1999 to 2000.

27. In 2024, playing for the Atlanta Falcons, DeAundre Alford made 72 tackles — only 76 men in the NFL made more that season. He played four seasons: for the Atlanta Falcons from 2021 to 2024 and the Winnipeg Blue Bombers in 2021. Over four seasons he made 174 tackles.

28. Tony Ramirez played in the NFL with the Detroit Lions (1997–99) and in the XFL with the Chicago Enforcers (2001). He did not play in 2000.

29. Arnold Campbell moved between leagues: the NFL with the Buffalo Bills (1987); the CFL with the Ottawa Rough Riders (1989). He did not play in 1988.

30. Starting with the St. Louis Cardinals in 1965, Ed McQuarters played ten seasons through 1974. He moved on to the Saskatchewan Roughriders from 1966 to 1974.

31. In 1983, playing for the Chicago Bears, Leslie Frazier intercepted 7 passes for 135 return yards — only thirteen men in the NFL intercepted more that season. He was later Head Coach of the Minnesota Vikings from 2010 to 2013.

32. Jacques Chapdelaine played seven seasons, beginning with the BC Lions in 1983, and was Head Coach of the Montreal Alouettes (earlier the Concordes) from 2016 to 2017. He moved on to the Montreal Alouettes from 1985 to 1986, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats from 1987 to 1989 and the Calgary Stampeders in 1989. Over seven seasons he caught 166 passes.

33. Jim Champion was a player first — two seasons from 1950 — and then Head Coach of the BC Lions from 1967 to 1969. He played for the New York Yanks from 1950 to 1951. He died on January 15, 1987.

34. Dutch Clark scored 73 points for the Detroit Lions in 1936; only one man in the NFL scored more that season. His seven seasons were with the Portsmouth Spartans from 1931 to 1932 and the Detroit Lions from 1934 to 1938. He did not play in 1933. He was later Head Coach of the Cleveland Rams from 1939 to 1942. He was a head coach at one other club too. In all he ran for 2,772 yards over six seasons. He died on August 5, 1978, in Canon City, CO.

35. Hugh Jernigan's career, two seasons in the USFL from 1983, began with the Philadelphia Stars. From there he went to the San Antonio Gunslingers in 1984.

36. Dick Zoll's career, three seasons in the NFL from 1937, began with the Cleveland Rams. From there he went to the Green Bay Packers in 1939. He died on September 6, 1985, in Green Bay, WI.

At North Carolina State, was All Conference two years and captain of the team for two years.”
