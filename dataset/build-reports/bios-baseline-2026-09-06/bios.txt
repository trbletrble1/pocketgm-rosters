1. Art Nowack's professional career was a single game, with the Ottawa Rough Riders in 1954.

2. Donte Paige-Moss's career crossed leagues: the CFL with the Toronto Argonauts (2012); ARENA with the Portland Thunder (2014) and the San Jose SaberCats (2015). He did not play in 2013.

3. Les Dodson's career was two games with the Wilmington Clippers in 1941. He was a United States Army veteran.

4. Roland Caranci played two professional games, both for the New York Giants in 1944. He served in the United States Army.

5. Alvin Ross moved between leagues: the NFL with the Philadelphia Eagles (1987); ARENA with the Pittsburgh Gladiators (1989). He did not play in 1988.

6. Dennis Houston's career was two games with the Dallas Cowboys in 2022.

7. Doug Ritchie played two professional games, both for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers in 1950.

8. One game was the whole of Mekhi Brown's career: the Tampa Bay Bandits, 2022, in the USFL.

9. Leon Winternheimer played two seasons for the Evansville Crimson Giants of the APFA, from 1921 to 1922.

10. Joe Spagna played two seasons for the Frankford Yellow Jackets, 1922 to 1923.

11. John Fahay played in the NFL with the Milwaukee Badgers (1925), the Racine Tornadoes (1926) and the Minneapolis Red Jackets (1929) and in the AFL with the Chicago Bulls (1926). He was out of the game from 1927 to 1928. He died on January 8, 1980, in Fort Lauderdale, FL.

12. Al McIntosh spent two seasons with the Providence Steam Roller, of the NFL, from 1925 to 1926.

13. Lou Merillat spent a single season, 1925, with the Canton Bulldogs. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

14. Stan Keck played two professional games, both for the Cleveland Indians in 1923. He died on January 20, 1951, in Pittsburgh, PA.

15. John Kovatch's last game before the war came in 1942 with the Washington Redskins; his next, in 1946, was with the same club. He played three seasons: for the Washington Redskins in 1942 and 1946 and the Green Bay Packers in 1947. In all he caught 18 passes over two seasons. He was a United States Marine Corps veteran.

16. In 1947, playing for the Pittsburgh Steelers, Steve Lach ran for 372 yards and 8 touchdowns — only ten men in the NFL ran for more that season. His three seasons were with the Chicago Cardinals in 1942 and the Pittsburgh Steelers from 1946 to 1947. He was out of the game from 1943 to 1945. “A halfback throughout his career, Steve will be running from the fullback berth this season and he shows promise of developing into a stellar performer in that role.” — the 1946 Steelers guide.

17. Dick Plasman caught 19 passes for 403 yards for the Chicago Bears in 1939; only eleven men in the NFL caught more that season. He played eight seasons: for the Chicago Bears in 1937–41 and 1944, the Chicago Cardinals from 1946 to 1947 and the Chicago Cardinals in 1947. He was in and out of the game, missing 1942–43 and 1945. He later coached, most often as Assistant Coach, with the Pittsburgh Steelers from 1958 to 1961. He served in the United States Army Air Forces.

18. Joe Gibson was with the Cleveland Rams in 1944, then out of football until 1946 and the Brooklyn Dodgers. His five seasons were with the Cleveland Rams in 1942 and 1944, the Washington Redskins in 1943 and the Brooklyn Dodgers from 1946 to 1947. He was a United States Navy veteran.

19. Hank Rockwell was with the Cleveland Rams in 1942, then out of football until 1946 and the Los Angeles Dons. He played six seasons: for the PFA:LA in 1939, the Cleveland Rams from 1940 to 1942 and the Los Angeles Dons in 1946 and 1948. He did not play in 1947. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

20. Gerry Organ scored 139 points for the Ottawa Rough Riders in 1983; only five men in the CFL scored more that season. He did not play in 1978. In all he scored 1,462 points over twelve seasons.

21. Starting with the New York Giants in 1943, Joe Sulaitis played eleven seasons through 1953. His clubs were the New York Giants in 1943–45 and 1947–53, the Boston Yanks in 1946 and the PFA:JC in 1946. He died on February 8, 1980, in Point Pleasant, NJ.

22. Jon Condo played fourteen seasons in the NFL, from 2005 to 2019, starting with the Dallas Cowboys. He moved on to the Oakland Raiders from 2007 to 2017, the Atlanta Falcons in 2018 and the San Francisco 49ers in 2019. He did not play in 2006.

23. Basil Bark played thirteen seasons in the CFL, from 1965 to 1977, starting with the Montreal Alouettes. From there he went to the Calgary Stampeders from 1970 to 1977.

24. Glenn Hyde's career ran fourteen seasons, 1974 to 1987, beginning with the Chicago Fire. He went on to seven more clubs: the Charlotte Hornets, the Denver Broncos, the Baltimore Colts, the Denver Gold, the Chicago Blitz, the Seattle Seahawks and the Kansas City Chiefs.

25. In 1955, playing for the Toronto Argonauts, Tom Dublinski completed 225 passes for 3,561 yards — only two men in the CFL completed more that season. Over ten seasons he played for five clubs: the Detroit Lions, the Toronto Argonauts, the New York Giants, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats and the Denver Broncos. He did not play in 1956.

26. Tim Rattay's career ran eleven seasons, 2000 to 2011, beginning with the San Francisco 49ers. He went on to three more clubs: the Tampa Bay Buccaneers, the Arizona Cardinals and the Las Vegas Locomotives. He did not play in 2008. He later coached, as Wide Receivers of the Las Vegas Locomotives from 2011 to 2012. He coached for three seasons in all. Over nine seasons he completed 524 passes.

27. Aaron Woods played in the UFL with the Sacramento Mountain Lions (2010–11) and the Las Vegas Locomotives (2012) and in the CFL with the Winnipeg Blue Bombers (2013–14).

28. Harold Smith was a player first — five seasons from 1985 — and coached after, most often as Receivers/Quarterbacks, with the Saskatchewan Roughriders from 2000 to 2004. He played for five clubs: the Saskatchewan Roughriders, the New England Steamrollers, the Edmonton Eskimos, the Denver Dynamite and the Albany Firebirds. He did not play in 1987. Over two seasons he completed 27 passes.

29. Rod Shoate played in the NFL with the New England Patriots (1975 and 1977–81) and in the USFL with the New Jersey Generals (1983) and the Memphis Showboats (1984). He was in and out of the game, missing 1976 and 1982. In all he intercepted 6 passes over four seasons.

30. Frank Adams moved between leagues: the CFL with the Toronto Argonauts (1995); ARENA with the Connecticut Coyotes (1996).

31. Frank Bausch was a player first — eight seasons from 1934 — and coached after, as Assistant Coach of the Philadelphia Eagles in 1941. His clubs were the Boston Redskins from 1934 to 1936, the Chicago Bears from 1937 to 1940 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1941. He died on April 6, 1976, in Wichita, KS.

32. Al Babartsky's last game before the war came in 1941 with the Chicago Cardinals; his next was in 1943, with the Chicago Bears. He played six seasons: for the Chicago Cardinals in 1938–39 and 1941 and the Chicago Bears from 1943 to 1945. He did not play in 1940. He later coached, as Line of the Chicago Bears in 1947. He died on December 29, 2002, in Kettering, OH.

33. Earl Klapstein was a player first — one season from 1946 — and coached after, as Defensive Line of the Green Bay Packers in 1956. He played for the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1946. He was a United States Navy veteran.

34. Pat Carter's career ran ten seasons, 1988 to 1997, beginning with the Detroit Lions. He went on to four more clubs: the Los Angeles Rams, the Houston Oilers, the St. Louis Rams and the Arizona Cardinals. He later coached, as Tight Ends of the Detroit Lions from 2006 to 2008. He coached for four seasons in all. In all he caught 107 passes over eight seasons.

35. Darrius Blevins spent a single season, 2000, with the St. Louis Rams.

36. Carroll Zaruba's career was the 1960 season with the Dallas Texans.

37. Jason Cabinda played six seasons in the NFL, 2018 to 2023, starting with the Oakland Raiders. He moved on to the Detroit Lions from 2019 to 2023. Over six seasons he made 36 tackles.

38. Rip Bachor played one professional game, for the Detroit Wolverines in 1928.

39. Moore played one professional game, for the Cleveland Indians in 1921.

40. Ken Sigaty spent two seasons with the Edmonton Eskimos, in 1964 and 1966. He did not play in 1965.

41. Ed Rhinelander's professional career was a single game, with the Hamilton Tiger-Cats in 1962.

42. Harry Badger spent a single season, 1947, with the Winnipeg Blue Bombers. He was a Royal Canadian Army veteran.

43. Jack Carpenter played eight seasons, beginning with the Buffalo Bills in 1947, and then coached, as Assistant Line of the Toronto Argonauts in 1954. He went on to three more clubs: the San Francisco 49ers, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats and the Toronto Argonauts. “Northeast High Columbia University and University of Michigan In high school he played Tackle for two years and won All City honors in 1940.” — the 1947 Bills guide.

44. Bill Johnson was a player first — nine seasons from 1948 — and then Head Coach of the Cincinnati Bengals from 1976 to 1978. He spent another thirty-two seasons as an assistant. He played for the San Francisco 49ers from 1948 to 1956. The 1948 49ers guide put it this way: “Graduated from Tyler, Texas High School in 1943 and entered Texas A & M. He left school to enlist in the Navy in 1944. He was discharged in 1946 and entered Tyler J.”

45. Lou Rymkus's last game before the war came in 1943 with the Washington Redskins; his next was in 1946, with the Cleveland Browns. His seven seasons were with the Washington Redskins in 1943 and the Cleveland Browns from 1946 to 1951. He was later Head Coach of the Houston Oilers from 1960 to 1961. He spent another ten seasons as an assistant and was a head coach at one other club too. He served in the United States Navy.

46. Frank Umont played two seasons for the New York Giants, 1944 to 1945. He died on June 20, 1991, in Fort Lauderdale, FL.

47. Joe Skladany was a player first — one season from 1934 — and coached after, as Ends of the Pittsburgh Pirates in 1947. He served in the United States Navy.

48. In 1942, playing for the Chicago Cardinals, Al Coppage caught 20 passes for 196 yards — only ten men in the NFL caught more that season. He played five seasons: for the Chicago Cardinals from 1940 to 1942, the Cleveland Browns in 1946 and the Buffalo Bills in 1947. He was out of the game from 1943 to 1945. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

49. Frank Kristufek spent two seasons with the Brooklyn Dodgers, of the NFL, from 1940 to 1941. He served in the United States Army.

50. In 1939, playing for the Pittsburgh Pirates (later the Steelers), Sam Boyd caught 21 passes for 423 yards — only eight men in the NFL caught more that season. He served in the United States Navy.
