1. Art Nowack's professional career was a single game, with the Ottawa Rough Riders in 1954.

2. Donte Paige-Moss's career crossed leagues: the CFL with the Toronto Argonauts (2012); ARENA with the ARENAPOT (2014) and the ARENASJS (2015). He did not play in 2013.

3. Les Dodson's career was two games with the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1941. He was a United States Army veteran.

4. Roland Caranci played two professional games, both for the New York Giants in 1944. He served in the United States Army.

5. Alvin Ross moved between leagues: the NFL with the Philadelphia Eagles (1987); ARENA with the ARENAPIG (1989). He did not play in 1988.

6. Dennis Houston's career was two games with the Dallas Cowboys in 2022.

7. Doug Ritchie played two professional games, both for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers in 1950.

8. One game was the whole of Mekhi Brown's career: the Tampa Bay Bandits, 2022, in the USFL.

9. Leon Winternheimer played two seasons for the Evansville Crimson Giants of the APFA, from 1921 to 1922.

10. John Fahay played in the NFL with the Milwaukee Badgers (1925), the Racine Tornadoes (1926) and the Minneapolis Red Jackets (1929) and in the AFL with the Chicago Bulls (1926). He was out of the game from 1927 to 1928. He died on January 8, 1980, in Fort Lauderdale, FL.

11. Al McIntosh spent two seasons with the Providence Steam Roller, of the NFL, from 1925 to 1926.

12. Lou Merillat spent a single season, 1925, with the Canton Bulldogs. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

13. Stan Keck played two professional games, both for the Cleveland Indians in 1923. He died on January 20, 1951, in Pittsburgh, PA.

14. Mal Bross moved between leagues: the AFL with the Wilson's Wildcats (1926); the NFL with the Green Bay Packers (1927). He died on February 8, 1989, in Seattle, WA.

15. Bert Kuczynski played for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and did not play again until 1946, when he joined the Philadelphia Eagles. He played two seasons: for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1946. He was a United States Navy veteran.

16. Nick Kerasiotis's last game before the war came in 1942 with the Chicago Bears; his next, in 1945, was with the same club. He served in the United States Navy.

17. Dixie Stokes was with the Detroit Lions in 1939, then out of football until 1943 and the Chicago Cardinals. His four seasons were with the Detroit Lions from 1937 to 1939 and the Chicago Cardinals in 1943.

18. Joe Gibson was with the Cleveland Rams in 1944, then out of football until 1946 and the Brooklyn Dodgers. His five seasons were with the Cleveland Rams in 1942 and 1944, the Washington Redskins in 1943 and the Brooklyn Dodgers from 1946 to 1947. He was a United States Navy veteran.

19. Hank Rockwell was with the Cleveland Rams in 1942, then out of football until 1946 and the Los Angeles Dons. He played five seasons: for the Cleveland Rams from 1940 to 1942 and the Los Angeles Dons in 1946 and 1948. He did not play in 1947. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

20. Al Wilson played fifteen seasons, every one of them for the BC Lions, from 1972 to 1986.

21. Wayne Hansen's career ran ten seasons, 1950 to 1960, beginning with the Chicago Bears. From there he went to the Dallas Cowboys in 1960. He did not play in 1959. In all he intercepted 6 passes over five seasons.

22. Starting with the New York Giants in 2005, Justin Tuck played eleven seasons through 2015. From there he went to the Oakland Raiders from 2014 to 2015. In all he made 436 tackles over eleven seasons.

23. From 1965 to 1976, twelve seasons, Al Ford played for nobody but the Saskatchewan Roughriders. In all he caught 261 passes over twelve seasons.

24. In 1984, playing for the Memphis Showboats, Gary Shirk caught 57 passes for 604 yards — only fifteen men in the USFL caught more that season. He played eleven seasons: for the Memphis Southmen from 1974 to 1975, the New York Giants from 1976 to 1982 and the Memphis Showboats from 1984 to 1985. He did not play in 1983.

25. In 1955, playing for the Toronto Argonauts, Tom Dublinski completed 225 passes for 3,561 yards — only two men in the CFL completed more that season. Over ten seasons he played for five clubs: the Detroit Lions, the Toronto Argonauts, the New York Giants, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats and the Denver Broncos. He did not play in 1956.

26. Orien Harris moved between leagues: the NFL with the Cleveland Browns (2006) and the Cincinnati Bengals (2008–09); the UFL with the Virginia Destroyers (2011). He was in and out of the game, missing 2007 and 2010. Over three seasons he made 22 tackles.

27. Mitchell Fraboni played in the NFL with the Denver Broncos (2022–24) and in the USFL with the Pittsburgh Maulers (2022).

28. In 1993, playing for the New Orleans Saints, Vaughan Johnson made 110 tackles — only 45 men in the NFL made more that season. He played eleven seasons: for the Jacksonville Bulls from 1984 to 1985, the New Orleans Saints from 1986 to 1993 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1994. Over nine seasons he made 669 tackles.

29. Hilee Taylor moved between leagues: the NFL with the Carolina Panthers (2008–09); the CFL with the Saskatchewan Roughriders (2013–14). He was out of the game from 2010 to 2012.

30. Ray Bentley's career ran ten seasons, 1983 to 1992, beginning with the Michigan Panthers. He moved on to the Oakland Invaders in 1985, the Buffalo Bills from 1986 to 1991 and the Cincinnati Bengals in 1992. Over six seasons he intercepted 9 passes.

31. Bill Austin was a player first — seven seasons from 1949 — and then Head Coach of the Pittsburgh Steelers from 1966 to 1968. He was a head coach at one other club too. His clubs were the New York Giants in 1949–50 and 1953–57. He was out of the game from 1951 to 1952. He served in the United States Army.

32. George Brancato was a player first — eight seasons from 1954 — and then Head Coach of the Ottawa Rough Riders from 1974 to 1984. He played for the Chicago Cardinals in 1954, the Montreal Alouettes in 1956 and the Ottawa Rough Riders from 1957 to 1962. He did not play in 1955. In all he ran for 288 yards over five seasons. He died on October 22, 2019.

33. In 1983, playing for the Chicago Bears, Leslie Frazier intercepted 7 passes for 135 return yards — only thirteen men in the NFL intercepted more that season. He was later Head Coach of the Minnesota Vikings from 2010 to 2013.

34. Jacques Chapdelaine played seven seasons, beginning with the BC Lions in 1983, and was Head Coach of the Montreal Alouettes (earlier the Concordes) from 2016 to 2017. He moved on to the Montreal Alouettes from 1985 to 1986, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats from 1987 to 1989 and the Calgary Stampeders in 1989. Over seven seasons he caught 166 passes.

35. Eddie Wall's professional career was a single game, with the Frankford Yellow Jackets in 1930.

36. Lee Woodruff's career, three seasons in the NFL from 1931, began with the Providence Steam Roller. From there he went to the Boston Braves in 1932 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1933. He served in the United States Army.

37. John Jewell spent three seasons with the New Jersey Generals, of the USFL, from 1983 to 1985.

38. Walt Uzdavinis's career was the 1937 season with the Cleveland Rams. He died on December 23, 1988, in Glastonbury, CT.

39. Jack Daniels played one professional game, for the Milwaukee Badgers in 1925. He died on July 15, 1977, in Evergreen Park, IL.

40. Roy Cameron's career ran from 1960 to 1966, six seasons, beginning with the BC Lions. He played for the BC Lions in 1960 and 1965–66, the Edmonton Eskimos in 1961 and the Saskatchewan Roughriders in 1962–63 and 1965. He did not play in 1964.

41. Peter Paliotti's professional career was a single game, with the Montreal Alouettes in 1972.

42. Sullivan played one professional game, for the Rock Island Independents in 1920.

43. Over two seasons Ralph Earhart scored 24 points for the Green Bay Packers. “First year with the Packers...An outstanding break-away back at Texas Tech for the last two seasons... Border conference sprint champion with official time of 9.7 seconds in 100-yard dash...” — the 1948 Packers guide.

44. Tony Adamle spent his whole career with the Cleveland Browns: six seasons, 1947 to 1954. He was out of the game from 1952 to 1953. Over four seasons he intercepted 7 passes. He served in the United States Army.

45. Evan Vogds played in the AAFC with the Chicago Rockets (1946–47) and in the NFL with the Green Bay Packers (1948–49). Has B.S. degree in physical education... Served in Navy as a second class specialist...”

46. Don Ettinger played in the NFL with the New York Giants (1948–50) and in the CFL with the Saskatchewan Roughriders (1951), the Toronto Argonauts (1952–53) and the Hamilton Tiger-Cats (1954). In all he intercepted 9 passes over three seasons. The 1948 Giants guide put it this way: “All-Big 6 tackle last year. Started as fullback. Also played guard and center. Still has speed of a back.”

47. John Prchlik played five seasons, every one of them for the Detroit Lions, from 1949 to 1953. He was a United States Navy veteran.

48. Basil Wilkerson played two seasons in the NFL, 1932 to 1934, starting with the Boston Braves. He moved on to the Staten Island Stapletons in 1932 and the St. Louis Gunners in 1934. He did not play in 1933. He was a United States Navy veteran.

49. Jim Tarr played one professional game, for the Cleveland Indians in 1931. He was a United States Army Air Forces; United States Air Force veteran.

50. Cliff Speegle played one season of professional football, 1945, for the Chicago Cardinals in the NFL. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.
