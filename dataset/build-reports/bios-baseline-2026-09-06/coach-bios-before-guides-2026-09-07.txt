TEN MEN THE MERGE PUT BACK TOGETHER
(the first four are the men Ryan named as at risk; the other six are a seeded draw)

1. In 1964, playing for the Chicago Bears, Mike Ditka caught 75 passes for 897 yards — only two men in the NFL caught more that season. He played twelve seasons: for the Chicago Bears from 1961 to 1966, the Philadelphia Eagles from 1967 to 1968 and the Dallas Cowboys from 1969 to 1972. He was later Head Coach of the Chicago Bears from 1982 to 1992.

2. Tom Landry intercepted 8 passes for 99 return yards for the New York Giants in 1952; only five men in the NFL intercepted more that season. His seven seasons were with the New York Yankees in 1949 and the New York Giants from 1950 to 1955. He was later Head Coach of the Dallas Cowboys from 1960 to 1988. He died on February 12, 2000.

3. Tom Flores was a player first — nine seasons from 1960 — and then Head Coach of the Oakland Raiders, later the Los Angeles Raiders, from 1979 to 1987. He had been an assistant for seven seasons before that and was a head coach at one other club too. He played for the Oakland Raiders in 1960–61 and 1963–66, the Buffalo Bills from 1967 to 1969 and the Kansas City Chiefs in 1969. He did not play in 1962. In all he completed 838 passes over ten seasons.

4. Marty Schottenheimer was a player first — seven seasons from 1965 — and then Head Coach of the Kansas City Chiefs from 1989 to 1998. He had been an assistant for ten seasons before that and was a head coach at four other clubs too. He played for the Buffalo Bills from 1965 to 1968, the Boston Patriots from 1969 to 1970 and the Portland Storm in 1974. He was out of the game from 1971 to 1973. Over four seasons he intercepted 6 passes. He died on February 8, 2021.

5. Mike McCormack's career ran ten seasons, 1951 to 1962, beginning with the New York Yanks. He moved on to the Cleveland Browns from 1954 to 1962. He was out of the game from 1952 to 1953. He was later Head Coach of the Philadelphia Eagles from 1973 to 1975. He spent another eleven seasons as an assistant and was a head coach at two other clubs too. He died on October 15, 2013.

6. In 1951, playing for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers, Neill Armstrong caught 56 passes for 1,024 yards — only two men in the CFL caught more that season. He played seven seasons: for the Philadelphia Eagles from 1947 to 1951 and the Winnipeg Blue Bombers in 1951 and 1953–54. He did not play in 1952. He was later Head Coach of the Edmonton Eskimos from 1964 to 1969. He died on August 10, 2016, in Hurst, TX.

7. Ray Perkins played five seasons, beginning with the Baltimore Colts in 1967, and was Head Coach of the Tampa Bay Buccaneers from 1987 to 1990. He spent another twelve seasons as an assistant and was a head coach at one other club too. Over five seasons he caught 93 passes. He died on December 9, 2020.

8. Tom Keane intercepted 11 passes for 118 return yards for the Baltimore Colts in 1953; only one man in the NFL intercepted more that season. Over eight seasons he played for four clubs: the Los Angeles Rams, the Dallas Texans, the Baltimore Colts and the Chicago Cardinals. He was later Head Coach of the Wheeling Ironmen from 1962 to 1964. He spent another twenty-three seasons as an assistant. He was a United States Navy veteran.

9. In 1958, playing for the Baltimore Colts, Carl Taseff intercepted 7 passes for 52 return yards — only five men in the NFL intercepted more that season. Over eleven seasons he played for four clubs: the Cleveland Browns, the Baltimore Colts, the Philadelphia Eagles and the Buffalo Bills. He did not play in 1952. He later coached, most often as Running Backs, with the Miami Dolphins from 1970 to 1993.

10. Walt Michaels played twelve seasons in professional football, from 1951 to 1963, starting with the Green Bay Packers. He moved on to the Cleveland Browns from 1952 to 1961 and the New York Jets in 1963. He did not play in 1962. He was later Head Coach of the New York Jets from 1977 to 1982. He had been an assistant for fifteen seasons before that and was a head coach at one other club too. Over seven seasons he intercepted 11 passes. He died on July 10, 2019.


TEN WHO COACHED AND WERE NEVER MERGED

11. Mike Nixon played two seasons, beginning with the Pittsburgh Pirates in 1935, and was Head Coach of the Washington Redskins from 1959 to 1960. He was a head coach at one other club too. He moved on to the Brooklyn Dodgers in 1942. He was out of the game from 1936 to 1941. He served in the United States Navy.

12. Swede Youngstrom was a player first — eight seasons from 1920 — and then Head Coach of the Frankford Yellow Jackets in 1927. He played for four clubs: the Buffalo All-Americans, the Canton Bulldogs, the Cleveland Bulldogs and the Frankford Yellow Jackets. He was a United States Navy veteran.

13. Frank Clair was a player first — one season from 1941 — and then Head Coach of the Ottawa Rough Riders from 1956 to 1969. He was a head coach at one other club too. His clubs were the Washington Redskins in 1941. He was a United States Army veteran.

14. Joe Schmidt played thirteen seasons, every one of them for the Detroit Lions, from 1953 to 1965. He came back as Head Coach of the Detroit Lions from 1967 to 1972. Over ten seasons he intercepted 24 passes. He died on September 11, 2024.

15. In 1974, playing for the Kansas City Chiefs, Emmitt Thomas intercepted 12 passes for 214 return yards — only one man in the NFL intercepted more that season. He later became Head Coach of the Atlanta Falcons in 2007. In all he intercepted 58 passes over twelve seasons.

16. Ray McLean scored 54 points for the Chicago Bears in 1942; only two men in the NFL scored more that season. He later became Head Coach of the Green Bay Packers in 1958. He was a head coach at one other club too. He died on March 4, 1964, in Ann Arbor, MI.

17. Norb Hecker intercepted 8 passes for 26 return yards for the Washington Redskins in 1956; only three men in the NFL intercepted more that season. Over eight seasons he played for four clubs: the Los Angeles Rams, the Toronto Argonauts, the Washington Redskins and the Hamilton Tiger-Cats. He was later Head Coach of the Atlanta Falcons from 1966 to 1968. He died on March 13, 2004.

18. Bob Brodhead played two seasons, beginning with the Saskatchewan Roughriders in 1959, and became Head Coach of the Portland Thunder in 1975. He moved on to the Buffalo Bills in 1960. In all he completed 69 passes over two seasons. He died on February 11, 1996.

19. Tommy Hughitt was a player first — five seasons from 1920 — and then Head Coach of the Buffalo All-Americans from 1920 to 1924. Over three seasons he scored 41 points. He died on December 27, 1961, in Bartow, FL.

20. Starting with the Kansas City Chiefs in 1985, Jonathan Hayes played twelve seasons through 1996. He moved on to the Pittsburgh Steelers from 1994 to 1996. He later became Head Coach of the St. Louis BattleHawks in 2020. Over twelve seasons he caught 153 passes.
