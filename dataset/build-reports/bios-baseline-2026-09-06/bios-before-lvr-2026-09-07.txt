2. Donte Paige-Moss played two professional games, both for the Toronto Argonauts in 2012.

3. Les Dodson's career was two games with the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1941. He was a United States Army veteran.

4. Roland Caranci played two professional games, both for the New York Giants in 1944. He served in the United States Army.

5. Alvin Ross's career was two games with the Philadelphia Eagles in 1987.

6. Dennis Houston's career was two games with the Dallas Cowboys in 2022.

7. Doug Ritchie played two professional games, both for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers in 1950.

8. One game was the whole of Mekhi Brown's career: the Tampa Bay Bandits, 2022, in the USFL.

9. Leon Winternheimer played two seasons for the Evansville Crimson Giants of the APFA, from 1921 to 1922.

10. John Fahay played in the NFL with the Milwaukee Badgers (1925), the Racine Tornadoes (1926) and the Minneapolis Red Jackets (1929) and in the AFL with the Chicago Bulls (1926). He was out of the game from 1927 to 1928. He died on January 8, 1980, in Fort Lauderdale, FL.

11. Al McIntosh spent two seasons with the Providence Steam Roller, of the NFL, from 1925 to 1926.

12. Lou Merillat spent a single season, 1925, with the Canton Bulldogs. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

13. Stan Keck played two professional games, both for the Cleveland Indians in 1923. He died on January 20, 1951, in Pittsburgh, PA.

14. Mal Bross moved between leagues: the AFL with the Wilson's Wildcats (1926); the NFL with the Green Bay Packers (1927). He died on February 8, 1989, in Seattle, WA.

15. Bert Kuczynski played for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and did not play again until 1946, when he joined the Philadelphia Eagles. He played two seasons: for the Detroit Lions in 1943 and the Philadelphia Eagles in 1946. He was a United States Navy veteran.

16. Nick Kerasiotis's last game before the war came in 1942 with the Chicago Bears; his next, in 1945, was with the same club. He served in the United States Navy.

17. Dixie Stokes was with the Detroit Lions in 1939, then out of football until 1943 and the Chicago Cardinals. His four seasons were with the Detroit Lions from 1937 to 1939 and the Chicago Cardinals in 1943.

18. Joe Gibson was with the Cleveland Rams in 1944, then out of football until 1946 and the Brooklyn Dodgers. His five seasons were with the Cleveland Rams in 1942 and 1944, the Washington Redskins in 1943 and the Brooklyn Dodgers from 1946 to 1947. He was a United States Navy veteran.

19. Hank Rockwell was with the Cleveland Rams in 1942, then out of football until 1946 and the Los Angeles Dons. He played five seasons: for the Cleveland Rams from 1940 to 1942 and the Los Angeles Dons in 1946 and 1948. He did not play in 1947. He was a United States Army Air Forces veteran.

20. Al Wilson played fifteen seasons, every one of them for the BC Lions, from 1972 to 1986.

21. Jerry Norton intercepted 10 passes for 96 return yards for the St. Louis Cardinals in 1960; only two men in the NFL intercepted more that season. Over eleven seasons he played for five clubs: the Philadelphia Eagles, the Chicago Cardinals, the St. Louis Cardinals, the Dallas Cowboys and the Green Bay Packers.

22. Jermon Bushrod played twelve seasons in the NFL, from 2007 to 2018, starting with the New Orleans Saints. He played for the New Orleans Saints in 2007–12 and 2018, the Chicago Bears from 2013 to 2015 and the Miami Dolphins from 2016 to 2017.

23. From 1965 to 1976, twelve seasons, Al Ford played for nobody but the Saskatchewan Roughriders. In all he caught 261 passes over twelve seasons.

24. Starting with the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1977, Dirt Winston played ten seasons through 1986. His clubs were the Pittsburgh Steelers in 1977–81 and 1985–86 and the New Orleans Saints from 1982 to 1985. In all he intercepted 13 passes over six seasons.

25. In 1955, playing for the Toronto Argonauts, Tom Dublinski completed 225 passes for 3,561 yards — only two men in the CFL completed more that season. Over ten seasons he played for five clubs: the Detroit Lions, the Toronto Argonauts, the New York Giants, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats and the Denver Broncos. He did not play in 1956.

26. In 2003, playing for the Green Bay Packers, Ahman Green ran for 1,883 yards and 15 touchdowns — only two men in the NFL ran for more that season. Over thirteen seasons he played for four clubs: the Seattle Seahawks, the Green Bay Packers, the Houston Texans and the Omaha Nighthawks.

27. David Rivers moved between leagues: the NFL with the Tampa Bay Buccaneers (2017–18); the XFL with the St. Louis BattleHawks (2020); the CFL with the Winnipeg Blue Bombers (2021); the USFL with the New Jersey Generals (2022). He did not play in 2019. In all he made 26 tackles over two seasons.

28. Kaipo McGuire played in the NFL with the Indianapolis Colts (1997–98) and in the XFL with the Birmingham Thunderbolts (2001). He was out of the game from 1999 to 2000.

29. Arnold Campbell moved between leagues: the NFL with the Buffalo Bills (1987); the CFL with the Ottawa Rough Riders (1989). He did not play in 1988.

30. Starting with the St. Louis Cardinals in 1965, Ed McQuarters played ten seasons through 1974. He moved on to the Saskatchewan Roughriders from 1966 to 1974.

31. In 1983, playing for the Chicago Bears, Leslie Frazier intercepted 7 passes for 135 return yards — only thirteen men in the NFL intercepted more that season. He was later Head Coach of the Minnesota Vikings from 2010 to 2013.

32. Jacques Chapdelaine played seven seasons, beginning with the BC Lions in 1983, and was Head Coach of the Montreal Alouettes (earlier the Concordes) from 2016 to 2017. He moved on to the Montreal Alouettes from 1985 to 1986, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats from 1987 to 1989 and the Calgary Stampeders in 1989. Over seven seasons he caught 166 passes.

33. Jim Champion was a player first — two seasons from 1950 — and then Head Coach of the BC Lions from 1967 to 1969. He played for the New York Yanks from 1950 to 1951. He died on January 15, 1987.

34. Dutch Clark scored 73 points for the Detroit Lions in 1936; only one man in the NFL scored more that season. His seven seasons were with the Portsmouth Spartans from 1931 to 1932 and the Detroit Lions from 1934 to 1938. He did not play in 1933. He was later Head Coach of the Cleveland Rams from 1939 to 1942. He was a head coach at one other club too. In all he ran for 2,772 yards over six seasons. He died on August 5, 1978, in Canon City, CO.

35. John Jewell spent three seasons with the New Jersey Generals, of the USFL, from 1983 to 1985.

36. Chuck Hanneman played five seasons in the NFL, 1937 to 1941, starting with the Detroit Lions. He moved on to the Cleveland Rams in 1941. In all he caught 35 passes over five seasons. He died on March 31, 1999, in Cadillac, MI.

37. Mac Brown's career was the 2023 season with the Orlando Guardians.

38. Dave Smigelsky moved between leagues: the NFL with the Atlanta Falcons (1982); the USFL with the Washington Federals (1984). He did not play in 1983. Over two seasons he punted 81 times.

39. Peter Paliotti's professional career was a single game, with the Montreal Alouettes in 1972.

40. Sullivan played one professional game, for the Rock Island Independents in 1920.

41. Peter Blackbear's career was two games with the Oorang Indians in 1923.

42. E. McComiskey spent a single season, 1949, with the Hamilton Wildcats.

43. Evan Vogds played in the AAFC with the Chicago Rockets (1946–47) and in the NFL with the Green Bay Packers (1948–49). The 1949 Packers guide put it this way: “Second year with the Packers...Starting guard at Wisconsin in 1940 and 1941...Of German descent...Membcr of the Chicago All-Star squad in 1946... Has B.S. degree in physical education... Served in Navy as a second class specialist...”

44. Don Ettinger played in the NFL with the New York Giants (1948–50) and in the CFL with the Saskatchewan Roughriders (1951), the Toronto Argonauts (1952–53) and the Hamilton Tiger-Cats (1954). In all he intercepted 9 passes over three seasons. The 1948 Giants guide put it this way: “All-Big 6 tackle last year. Started as fullback. Also played guard and center. Still has speed of a back.”

45. Homer Hobbs played for the San Francisco 49ers in the AAFC (1949) and the NFL (1950). “Despite the Bulldogs’ loss in the ‘Hangover Day’ event, Coach Wally Butts signalled out the line play of Hobbs as outstanding, not only in Miami but throughout the 1948 season.” — the 1949 49ers guide.

46. Paul Gibson played in the AAFC with the Buffalo Bills (1947–49) and in the CFL with the Ottawa Rough Riders (1950) and the Hamilton Tiger-Cats (1951). In all he caught 22 passes over three seasons. The 1949 Bills guide put it this way: “In high school, won letters in football, basketball and baseball. At North Carolina State, was All Conference two years and captain of the team for two years.”

48. Hal Robl played two professional games, both for the Chicago Cardinals in 1945. He was a United States Army veteran.

49. Ray Whipple played one season of professional football, 1920, for the Detroit Heralds in the APFA. He served in the United States Army.

50. Joe Skladany spent a single season, 1934, with the Pittsburgh Pirates. He served in the United States Navy.
