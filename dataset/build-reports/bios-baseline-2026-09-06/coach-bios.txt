TEN MEN THE MERGE PUT BACK TOGETHER
(the first four are the men Ryan named as at risk; the other six are a seeded draw)

1. In 1964, playing for the Chicago Bears, Mike Ditka caught 75 passes for 897 yards — only two men in the NFL caught more that season. He played twelve seasons: for the Chicago Bears from 1961 to 1966, the Philadelphia Eagles from 1967 to 1968 and the Dallas Cowboys from 1969 to 1972. He was later Head Coach of the Chicago Bears from 1982 to 1992.

2. Tom Landry intercepted 8 passes for 99 return yards for the New York Giants in 1952; only five men in the NFL intercepted more that season. His seven seasons were with the New York Yankees in 1949 and the New York Giants from 1950 to 1955. He was later Head Coach of the Dallas Cowboys from 1960 to 1988. He died on 2/12/2000.

3. Tom Flores was a player first — nine seasons from 1960 — and then Head Coach of the Oakland Raiders, later the Los Angeles Raiders, from 1979 to 1987. He had been an assistant for seven seasons before that and was a head coach at one other club too. He played for the Oakland Raiders in 1960–61 and 1963–66, the Buffalo Bills from 1967 to 1969 and the Kansas City Chiefs in 1969. He did not play in 1962. In all he completed 838 passes over ten seasons.

4. Marty Schottenheimer was a player first — seven seasons from 1965 — and then Head Coach of the Kansas City Chiefs from 1989 to 1998. He had been an assistant for ten seasons before that and was a head coach at four other clubs too. He played for three clubs: the Buffalo Bills, the Boston Patriots and the Portland Storm. He was out of the game from 1971 to 1973. Over four seasons he intercepted 6 passes. He died on February 8, 2021.

5. Dick Nolan was a player first — nine seasons from 1954 — and then Head Coach of the San Francisco 49ers from 1968 to 1975. He spent another seventeen seasons as an assistant and was a head coach at one other club too. His clubs were the New York Giants in 1954–57 and 1959–61, the Chicago Cardinals in 1958 and the Dallas Cowboys in 1962. Over seven seasons he intercepted 23 passes. He died on November 11, 2007.

6. In 1953, playing for the Winnipeg Blue Bombers, Bud Grant caught 68 passes for 922 yards — only one man in the CFL caught more that season. He played six seasons: for the Philadelphia Eagles from 1951 to 1952 and the Winnipeg Blue Bombers from 1953 to 1956. He was later Head Coach of the Minnesota Vikings from 1967 to 1983. He was a head coach at two other clubs too. He died on March 11, 2023.

7. Ray Prochaska was a player first — one season from 1941 — and then Head Coach of the St. Louis Cardinals in 1961. He spent another thirty seasons as an assistant. His clubs were the Cleveland Rams in 1941. He served in the United States Army.

8. Ernie Stautner played fourteen seasons, every one of them for the Pittsburgh Steelers, from 1950 to 1963. He was later Head Coach of the FRA from 1995 to 1997. He had been an assistant for twenty-nine seasons before that.

9. In 1958, playing for the Baltimore Colts, Carl Taseff intercepted 7 passes for 52 return yards — only five men in the NFL intercepted more that season. Over eleven seasons he played for four clubs: the Cleveland Browns, the Baltimore Colts, the Philadelphia Eagles and the Buffalo Bills. He did not play in 1952. He later coached, most often as Offensive Backs, with the Miami Dolphins from 1970 to 1993.

10. Walt Michaels played twelve seasons in professional football, from 1951 to 1963, starting with the Green Bay Packers. He moved on to the Cleveland Browns from 1952 to 1961 and the New York Jets in 1963. He did not play in 1962. He was later Head Coach of the New York Jets from 1977 to 1982. He had been an assistant for fifteen seasons before that and was a head coach at one other club too. Over seven seasons he intercepted 11 passes. He died on July 10, 2019.


TEN WHO COACHED AND WERE NEVER MERGED

11. In 1994, playing for the New England Patriots, Ben Coates caught 96 passes for 1,174 yards — only four men in the NFL caught more that season. His ten seasons were with the New England Patriots from 1991 to 1999 and the Baltimore Ravens in 2000. He later coached, as Tight Ends of the Cleveland Browns from 2005 to 2006.

12. Jack Lawrence was a player first — two seasons from 1945 — and coached after, as Line of the Calgary Stampeders in 1946. He played for the Calgary Stampeders from 1945 to 1946 and the Calgary Stampeders in 1946.

13. Frank Souchak played one season, beginning with the Pittsburgh Pirates in 1939, and then coached, their Ends, in 1946. He was a United States Navy veteran.

14. Jake Moreland played two seasons, beginning with the New York Jets in 2000, and then coached, as Tight Ends of the Houston Texans from 2023 to 2024. He coached for five seasons in all. From there he went to the Cleveland Browns in 2001.

15. Ken Houston played fourteen seasons in professional football, from 1967 to 1980, starting with the Houston Oilers. From there he went to the Washington Redskins from 1973 to 1980. He later coached, as Defensive Backs of the HOU from 1984 to 1985. He coached for four seasons in all. In all he intercepted 49 passes over twelve seasons.

16. Sam Anno was a player first — seven seasons from 1987 — and coached after, as Defensive Assistant of the Oakland Raiders from 2015 to 2017. He played for four clubs: the Los Angeles Rams, the Minnesota Vikings, the Tampa Bay Buccaneers and the San Diego Chargers.

17. Marcus Brady was a player first — seven seasons from 2002 — and coached after, most often as Offensive Coordinator, with the Indianapolis Colts from 2018 to 2022. He coached for seventeen seasons in all. His clubs were the Toronto Argonauts from 2002 to 2003, the Hamilton Tiger-Cats from 2004 to 2005 and the Montreal Alouettes from 2006 to 2008. Over seven seasons he completed 246 passes.

18. Bob Mischak played seven seasons, beginning with the New York Giants in 1958, and then coached, most often as Tight Ends/Strength and Conditioning, with the Oakland Raiders, later the Los Angeles Raiders, from 1980 to 1987. He coached for ten seasons in all. He moved on to the New York Titans from 1960 to 1962 and the Oakland Raiders from 1963 to 1965. He did not play in 1959.

19. Mel Doherty was a player first — one season from 1921 — and then Head Coach of the Cincinnati Celts in 1921. He died on July 17, 1942.

20. Starting with the Cleveland Browns in 1967, Bob Matheson played thirteen seasons through 1979. From there he went to the Miami Dolphins from 1971 to 1979. He later coached, most often as Special Teams, with the Miami Dolphins from 1983 to 1986. In all he intercepted 12 passes over eight seasons.
