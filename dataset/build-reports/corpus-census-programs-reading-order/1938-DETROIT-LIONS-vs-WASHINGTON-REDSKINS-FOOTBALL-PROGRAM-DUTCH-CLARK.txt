EDWARD L. KLEWICKI, of the Hamtramck Klewikis is the original "One-Man Gang".
Ed starred at end for Coach Charley Bachman at Michigan State College for three years and
joined up with the Lions in 1936 where he immediately filled the bill. Coach Dutch Clark
rates him unequalled as a defensive end, not even excepting famed Bill Hewitt, now of the
Philadelphia Eagles.
At Cranbrook, the chief topic of conversation among the freshman candidates was
Klewicki and how to block him. One of them asked Coach Clark. Dutch replied,
me, I never blocked him. Thank heaven."
Klewicki is 27, Polish and married. He is interested in chemistry and will devote all his
time to the automobile industry when his playing days are over.
RAYMOND J. (BUTCH) MORSE is starting his fourth season at end for the Lions.
From his home city of Portland, Oregon, Morse entered Oregon University and made a
name for himself on the Pacific Coast as a fullback as well as end. His six-foot-two frame
and 206 pounds made him a natural end in pro football and that is where he has played.
Before reporting to the Lions in 1935, Butch coached Mejii University in Japan during
the winter of 1934-1935,

marriage as well as adoption. He induced Miss Wilma Kerwin to become Mrs. Christensen
on June 1. He has the distinction of being the biggest as well as the oldest player in point
of service on the Lions' roster. Although the bridegroom is only 29, he played three years
at Portsmouth, O. and is starting his fifth season in Detroit.
The big tackle was handicapped last year by starting in mid-season. This year he started
with the rest from taw and his 250 pounds are honest muscle, topped with one of the

parental home. He is one of the three men elected this year as a field leader. The others are
Caddel and Johnson.

JOHN DENVIL (JACK) JOHNSON, the leader of the Lion line, is starting his fifth
season as a tackle for Detroit. Jack and Detroit made their professional football debut
simultancously. He came here from University of Utah in 1934, the year the National
Football League franchise was transferred from Portsmouth, O. One of the greatest defensive
tackles in football history, Jack can impart his knowledge as well as use it. He coaches the
line for Dutch Clark. Johnson will be 29 in November, is married and a member of the
Church of Latter Day Saints. When not playing football Jack ranches at his home mailing
point, Grantsville, Utah. On the pro footbali fields his six-fect-three frame and 220 pounds
of brawn are as much a landmark as the Mormon temple in Salt Lake City.

the Pacific slope.

MAURICE PATT is the Hank Greenberg of the Lions, a Jewish athlete who by the appli-
cation of intelligence, ambition and determination has won himself a high place in Big
Time sports. A three-sport man at Altoona high school in Pennsylvania, Patt cut his activi-
ties to football and basketball at Carnegie Tech and starred in both. He won All-America
rating at end and was captain of the Tech basketball team.
Patt is 23 years old, 6 feet, two inches tall and weighs 207 pounds. In making his profes-
sional debut he is assured a distinguished career as a Lion. He is an exceptional pass catcher
as well as a smart defensive end. As a collegian he averaged five completed passes a gamc
igainst such teams as Notre Dame, Temple, New York University, Holy Cross, Duquesne
and Pittsburgh.
JACK MACKENROTH, freshman center from North Dakota University can scarcely
wait until the Lions move into Briggs Stadium. Reason: He never has escaped injury in
U. of D. Stadium. As the star lineman of North Dakota he sprained an ankle, wrenched
a back in two games played there against Gus Dorais' Titans. In the annual Lions' Freshman.
Varsity game there this Fall, Jack tore a ligament in his left knee, the only major casualty.
l'hus he starts the season on crutches. But in the limited time and opportunity at his dis-
posal, the 22-year-old lineman won a place on the roster. He is the utility man of the line,
playing center, guard or tackle with equal facility and skill. He is single and makes his
iome at Minot, N. D., is six feet, two inches tall, weighs 21).
LESTER JAMES GRAHAM is a 215-pound guard from the oil fields around Tulsa,
Oklahoma. A superb blocker, Graham was an All-Missouri Valley, Conference selection
while playing for Tulsa University, He majored in business administration, intent upon
entering the oil business with his father and it required some persuasion to induce him to
quit the oil fields for the professional football fields. Now that he has made the Lions' team
he has no regrets. Graham was born at Hominy, Okla., July 1, 1916 and still calls Hominy
home. When he confessed to no nickname, Miss Rozene Richards, daughter of the Lions'
owner, promptly dubbed him "Cracker". And "Cracker" it is.

LLOYD R. (CYCLONE) CARDWELL, whirled out of his native Nebraska in 1937 to
win himself a job in the Lions' backfield. Playing right halfback for Nebraska he laid waste
some of the best teams in the Big Ten, Big Six as well as non-numerical defenses of various
Cardwell, at 25, has his best years of professional football ahead. He was hampered some-
what in his debut by an injured knee, but reported this year sound and rarin' to go. Breezy
as a prairie wind, a natural mimic, Cardwell is as much a favorite with his team mates as
ALEXANDER FRANCIS (WOJIE) WOJCIECHOWICZ was All-American center
for Coach Jimmy Crowley's Fordham eleven for two years before he signed a Lion contract
chis year to make his professional debut. Wojie was Coach Dutch Clark's first choice in the
annual draft. The East thought so much of him he was the first man signed up to play in
the Eastern All-College Stars vs. New York Giants game in the Polo Grounds. Wojie made
three round-trip airplane trips between here and New York in order to learn signals under
both Andy Kerr and Coach Clark. He is 23 years old, six feet tall and weighs an even 200
pounds. Wojie is married and his biggest thrill, next to signing a Lion contract, was
announcing the birth of Alexander Francis Wojciechowicz, Jr., born Aug. 19, 1938.
WILLIAM L. (BILL) SHEPHERD came to the Lions via Western Maryland College
with a brief stop-over at Boston. Bill played part of the 1935 season with the Boston Red-
skins after attracting notice as a member of the College All-Star team which met Chicago
Bears in Chicago before the league schedules started.
Shepherd is 27, married and the father of two husky sons. Although he is only 5 feet,
nine inches tall, Bill packs 205 pounds on his sturdy legs. He is one of the hardest men in
football to tackle, a first rate passer and kicker. He can play quarterback or fullback and
do a stint at blocking when called upon, the handy man of the backfield.

JAMES A. (JIM) McDONALD is the third member of the Lions' triumvirate from Ohio
State. Jim is 22 years old, six feet, one inch and weighs 195. He was purchased by the Lions
rom Cleveland Rams, who obtained him from Philadelphia Eagles. Bert Bell, of the Eagles
was entitled to first choice in the annual draft meeting and McDonald was his selection.
McDonald starred in the annual All-Star game at Chicago and repeated with trimmings
in an all-star game against the Cleveland Rams in Cleveland, Sept. 2. He was captain and
quarterback of the Buckeyes last season, majored in physical education and is a member of
sigma Chi fraternity. His home is Springfield, O.
WILLIAM (BILL) RADOVICH is the "Human Depth Bomb" of the Lion line. This
§ feet, nine inch, 235-pound guard from Southern California is so much the football type
he has appeared in football pictures at three studios on the Pacific Coast-Metro-Goldwyn-
Twentieth Century-Fox and Paramount.
In three seasons at guard for the Trojans, Bill made virtually every all-opponents' poll and
won top rating from such coaches as Robert Zuppke, James Phelan and Elmer Layden.
Bill has an exceptionally fast charge and loves the rough going. He is 23, a member of
California.
Sigma Chi fraternity. To gain his degree, Bill must attend one more semester at Southern
JAMES HENDERSON (MONK) MOSCRIP is one of "the laughing boys of Stanford",
the name bestowed upon the Stanford squad by an eastern sports writer who visited Palo
Alto in 1934 before the Rose Bowl game with Columbia. At left end, Monk was the most
light-hearted of all. He played in three Rose Bowl games-against Columbia in 1934,
Alabama in 1935 and Southern Methodist in 1936. He was a team mate of Tackle Bob
Reynolds who joined the Lions last season. Monk stayed away a year after winning All-
American honors in 1934 and 1935 and All-Pacific Coast selection three years in a row.
But the lure of the Lions proved too strong and he reported this Fall. Monk was born in
Adena, O., where his parents still reside, Sept. 17, 1913. He graduated from Stanford with
a bachelor of arts degree and is a member of Zeta Psi fraternity.

backfield performer in football.

Gutowsky is married and lives in Kingfisher, Okla, where in the oft-season he is employed
in the oil fields.
Starting his seventh season in professional football he is established as one of the game's
greatest line plungers.

great runner, the ideal triple-threat back.
