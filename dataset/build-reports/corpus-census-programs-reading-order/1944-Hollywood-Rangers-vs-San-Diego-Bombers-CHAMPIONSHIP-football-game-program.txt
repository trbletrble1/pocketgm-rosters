Outstanding Bomber Stars
STEVE BAGARUS, product of Notre Dame, has been the
main cog in the amazing success of the San Diego Bombers this
season. He looks like a gridiron Frank Sinatra-lean and hungry.
6 feet tall, weighing a little less than 170 pounds- but he's as
slippery as the proverbial eel, and his spectacular dashes down
an open field have made him the idol of San Diego grid fans.
Steve is under contract to the Washington Redskins; you'll hear
more about him, after the war, when he reports to Frank Marshall.
SAL MENA. 235-pound former Trojan, played on the same
team with several of the Ranger stars at college. Sal played end
at U.S.C., but Coach Larry Hanson of the Bombers has found him
an ace tackle, and Sal has started every game this year for San
Diego.
HAL HIGGINS, bald of pate but lightning fast, is the first-
string quarterback for the Bombers. He set prep school records
for the sprints and hurdles at La Jolla School, went on to become
a grid hero at Oregon State. Hal is the Pavelec of San Diego; he
has kicked 37 placements this year, only 2 less than the record
set by Bob Snyder of the Chicago Bears for the National League
in 1943.
MULE ZLAPOVICH hails from St. Mary's College, Texas-
and he wasn't called "Mule" for nothing. He's a rough and ready
gent, an outstanding guard, and has figured prominently in
Bomber victories this year.
HAL KARNS comes from Marquette; he's the 200-pound full-
back who distinguished himself in the game with the Los Angeles
AIl-Stars a few weeks back. Kamns hits the line like a General
Grant tank, and makes impressive yardage every time he smacks
