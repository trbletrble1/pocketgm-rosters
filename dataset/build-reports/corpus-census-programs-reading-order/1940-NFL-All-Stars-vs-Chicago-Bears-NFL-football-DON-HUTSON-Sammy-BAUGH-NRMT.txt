Halas. Ever since 1914-18 when he was playing right

with the football bug.

As a member of the Great Lakes squad he

Only once in his meteoric career in the football
world did George "get out of character." He took a
fling at baseball in 1920, joining the New York
Yankees as an outfielder. However, his diamond dust-
ing carcer ended as abruptly as it opened when he

and direction of football, baseball, and basketball in
the big commercial circuits then in full swing
through the midwest.
The following year Mr. Big of the Big Bears
joined with "Dutch" Sternaman in moving the
Staley's football squad to Chicago where the now
famous Chicago Bears came into being. The first
squad included a number of Halas' former team
mates on the Great Lakes squad as well as some ex-
cellent Staley's material and what gridiron talent was
left lying around after the other nineteen pro clubs

George and Sternaman coached and played until
1928 when Halas dropped out to the sidelines to get

business enterprises. At the opening of the 1929 sea-
son, Ralph Jones was engaged to coach the Bears, and
for the next three seasons he kept the Chicago pro

filled at the opening of the 1933 season when he again
took over the reins as Mr. Big.
