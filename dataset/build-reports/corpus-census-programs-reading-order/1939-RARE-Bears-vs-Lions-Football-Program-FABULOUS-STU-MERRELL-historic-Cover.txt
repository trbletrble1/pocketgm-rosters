Jack Manders is pushing Ken Strong in the
race for life-time scoring honors, trailing the
eastern star's 329 points by eight.
Manders, who is playing his seventh season
with the club and who, with Musso, shares the
distinction of being the only active survivors of
the championship outfit of 1933, already owns
three league marks. In 1934 the greatest place-
ment kicker in the history of the game, scored
79 points. Thirty-one of them were points after
touchdowns, a record, and ten field goals in-
cluded, are also a record.
During that season he booted 72 consecutive
conversions, which beyond a doubt is a world
mark.
In the championship playoff with the New
York Giants in 1933, which the Bears won, 23
to 21, Manders booted three field goals, a feat
not equalled in a championship game but equalled
by Cuff of the Giants against the Bears last
Sunday.
In that same year Beattie Feathers, playing
his initial campaign carried the ball 1,004 yards
in 101 tries from scrimmage to average 10 yards
in eleven games. Both yardage and average are
tops for the circuit.

by Rocky Wolfe
Roy "Link" Lyman, now in his fifth season
as assistant coach at Nebraska, played twelve
years in the pro circuit of which eight were
with the Bears, which, combined with his four
terms at Nebraska, gave him a total of 217 hours
of competition in 292 games, when he retired
at the close of the 1934 schedule.
George Trafton gave the centers a mark at
which to shoot when he retired at the finish of
the 1933 season with thirteen years to his credit.
He played in 201 games, 158 hours of actual
play and used but one hand in snapping the
ball. He is one of the original members of the
club, organized in Decatur, IlI., in 1920 by Coach
George Halas and known as the Staleys.
Bronko Nagurski owns the all-time individual
yardage record of 3,947 made in eight years as
a fullback. His total would have been much
larger had he not been used frequently as a
b'ocker. (He did the blocking when Feathers
established his record, and had he not been
handicapped by illness one season which kept
him out of the majority of games.)
The big fellow, generally regarded as the
hardest hitting man in the post-graduate ranks,
carried the oval 856 times to average 4.6 yards,
which is something of a mark in itself for a
plunging fullback.
