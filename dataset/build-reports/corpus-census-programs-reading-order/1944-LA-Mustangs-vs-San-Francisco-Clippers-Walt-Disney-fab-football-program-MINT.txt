JIM BAYLESS, tackle, 6 ft., 200 lbs.
Tim Bayless comes to pro football from Compton I. C.
He is a good all-around end and is adept at place-
ment kicking or kicking off. Maybe that useful right
hoof of his is one reason why he lists big-footed
Ernie Smith as the best player he ever saw. Jim is
23 and was born at Houston, Idaho.
BOB LIBBEE, halfback, 5 ft. 10 in., 180 lbs.
One of the stars of the first scrimmage of the season
held before the player draft landed him with the
Mustangs, libbee has from the very start been
pegged as one of the boys to watch in the 1944 Ameri-
can league play. Libbee is 24, was born at Selma,
played for Frank Alustiza at Sacramento High, for
Lon Stiner at Oregon State and for Gil Kuhn at Lowry
Field. He played in the transplanted Rose Bowl game
at Duke on Jan. 1, 1942, and rates that Duke outfit the
best he ever ran up against. He says that Frankie
Albert was the best player he ever faced. Libbee
kicks and passes from the right, plays safety, is alert
on pass defense, shows both speed and line smashing
ability as a ball carrier and is willing and able to
do his full share of blocking and tackling.

has been burning up the gridiron for the Mustangs.
He can toss a football with "on the spot" accuracy
75 yards, and then turn right around and make circus
catches on the receiving end. His teammates know

It was the first day of practice, before the player draft, and a group of Los
Angeles sports writers turned out at Griffith Park Playground to see what kind
of talent would show up for the three local American league teams. Of more
than 80 candidates, the man who attracted the most attention among the scribes
was an unknown, long-armed, loose-limbed Negro who, with supreme non-
chalance, was calmly throwing footballs nearly the length of the field.
The scribes decided to call him "Murphy," but it turned out that his name
is Ezzrett Anderson, that in his home state of Arkansas he is known as "Lil'
Abner," and that in California it has just been plain Andy. So soon after he
was drafted by the Mustangs his teammates decided to call him "Sugarfoot"
and they say he's about as sweet an all-around performer as pro football this
season will produce.
Sugarfoot wears size 14 carpet slippers instead of shoes when he shuffles
about town, has feet so flat that beside them a Sherman tank would appear to
have high insteps, and in his every movement or gesture he carries himself as
loosely as a sack of grain with rubber arms and legs attached. He constantly
sways from port to starboard like a ship in a high gale and he is the most relaxed
athlete ever seen in any sport in Los Angeles.
He can throw a football with deadly accuracy 75 or 80 yards and when he
recovers a loose ball he is not so liable to fall on it as he is to scoop it up with
one hand, as if it were a baseball, and start running with it. Bom 24 years ago
in Nashville, Ark., he was at various times all-city and all-state tackle, end and
fullback at Langston High, Hot Springs, Ark.
Later he was star tackle at Kentucky State College, a Negro school located
at Frankfort, Ky., and he has performed locally for the East Side Wildcats.
He is an agile, nimble and long-armed tackler, is good at smashing the
interference, blocks kicks, can receive as well as throw passes and is expected
to become the most colorful and perhaps the most popular box office attraction
of the 1944 American league season. Anderson's father, Ezzrett, Sr., was for
many years catcher for the New York Black Yankees baseball team.
The Sugarfoot rates Snaky Smith of Arkansas State College the greatest
player he ever played against and thinks Don Hutson the best he has ever seen.
He has been playing football since he was 14 years of age but this is the first
time he has had a chance to prove his ability in big-time competition.
This means he is competing with and against far more experienced players
and undoubtedly he has much to learn. He may make many mistakes in his
fans will love him.

MILTON POLLACK, tackle, 6 ft 2½ in., 215 Ibs.
Once a member of Califomia's Rose Bowl Golden
Bears, Milt Pollack is now in his seventh year of pro
tootball. He played four years with the L. A. Bui.
dogs, one each with the Columbus Bulls and L A
Mustangs. He is well tuned to Dan Bamnharts syslen
and prefers it to any other he has ever been exposed
Milt is 29 years old, was born at Des Moires,
lowa, and attended Hollywood High. His is a slast-
ing style of play and in his greatest moments he has
risen to heights seldom surpassed by any westen
lineman.
WADE WILLEY, halfback, 5 ft. 10 in., 167 Ibs.
Formerly a second lieutenant in the Anti-Aircraft.
from which service he was honorably discharged,
Wade Willey brings to the Mustang roster ceriain
qualities of leadership which distinguished his play
at Los Angeles High and U.S.C. He was an all-city
and all-Southern California prep school star and is
adept at ball carrying and pass receiving. He has
played both single and double wing systems, prefers
single wing and is playing wingback for Dan Bam-
hart. Willey was born in Iowa and is 28 years old.
FRANK DARRAH, guard, 5 ft. 7 in., 180 lbs.
"Red" Darrah was one of Chief Newman's stars at
Whittier College before he joined the L. A. Bulldogs
and then the Mustangs. He is a good drop kicker.
He was born 28 years ago at Chazy, N. Y., and at-
tended Fairfax High in Los Angeles.
JOSEPH DIXON, center, 6 ft. 1 in., 201 lbs.
Known to his pals as "Colonel Joe," Dixon was a star
center for Frank Thomas at Alabama and was a mem-
ber of the South team in the NorhSouth game of
1939. He likes to back up the line, where he shows
to advantage in spotting his opponents' olays, and
in pass defense. Formerly in the Marines Col. Joe
later joined the Navy Air Corps and was discharged
because of a bad knee. He rates Bob Masterson of
the Washington Redskins the best he ever plaved
with or against. Born in Washington, Indiana
years ago, Dixon attended Warren High, Warren,
Ohio, and then Alabama. He played with the Mus-
tangs in 1943.
