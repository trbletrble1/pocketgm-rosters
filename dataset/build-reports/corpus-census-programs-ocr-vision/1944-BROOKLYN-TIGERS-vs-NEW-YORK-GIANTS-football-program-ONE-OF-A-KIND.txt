THERE are backfield superlatives on the New York foorball club. Those of
you who have been watching Steve Owen's club all season know how Bill
Paschal, Howie Livingston, Arnie Herber, Ward Cuff, et al, have been turning
in amazing feats week after week.
But, as Stour Steve likes to keep saying, "Nearly all football games are won or
lost up front. The line,—that poor, old ignored line,—has to dish it out and
take it."
Owen's line veterans have been magnificent. The great triumph over the Packers
last week, 24 to 0, showed Mel Hein and Len Younce at their best.
The West Point football squad warched that contest, and when it was all over,
the Cadets exclaimed, "Boy, that Younce man is one whale of a guard! And how
does that Hein, at the age of 35, do the things he accomplished this afternoon?"
It's a matter of skill, and spirit, the will to win and the know-how.
Younce is not only a great lineman, but has the distinction of being a remark-
able kicker. He comes out of that line to do the punting and can boot the pigskin
with the best of them.
