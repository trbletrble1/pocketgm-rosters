Ezzrett Anderson
End, 6 feet 3½ inches, 205 pounds
It was the first day of practice, before the player draft, and a group of Los
Angeles sports writers turned out at Griffith Park Playground to see what kind
of talent would show up for the three local American league teams. Of more
than 80 candidates, the man who attracted the most attention among the scribes
was an unknown, long-armed, loose-limbed Negro who, with supreme non-
chalance, was calmly throwing footballs nearly the length of the field.
The scribes decided to call him "Murphy," but it turned out that his name
is Ezzrett Anderson, that in his home state of Arkansas he is known as "Lil'
Abner," and that in California it has just been plain Andy. So soon after he
was drafted by the Mustangs his teammates decided to call him "Sugarfoot"
and they say he's about as sweet an all-around performer as pro football this
season will produce.
Sugarfoot wears size 14 carpet slippers instead of shoes when he shuffles
about town, has feet so flat that beside them a Sherman tank would appear to
have high insteps, and in his every movement or gesture he carries himself as
loosely as a sack of grain with rubber arms and legs attached. He constantly
sways from port to starboard like a ship in a high gale and he is the most relaxed
athlete ever seen in any sport in Los Angeles.
He can throw a football with deadly accuracy 75 or 80 yards and when he
recovers a loose ball he is not so liable to fall on it as he is to scoop it up with
one hand, as if it were a baseball, and start running with it. Bom 24 years ago
in Nashville, Ark., he was at various times all-city and all-state tackle, end and
fullback at Langston High, Hot Springs, Ark.
Later he was star tackle at Kentucky State College, a Negro school located
at Frankfort, Ky., and he has performed locally for the East Side Wildcats.
He is an agile, nimble and long-armed tackler, is good at smashing the
interference, blocks kicks, can receive as well as throw passes and is expected
to become the most colorful and perhaps the most popular box office attraction
of the 1944 American league season. Anderson's father, Ezzrett, Sr., was for
many years catcher for the New York Black Yankees baseball team.
The Sugarfoot rates Snaky Smith of Arkansas State College the greatest
player he ever played against and thinks Don Hutson the best he has ever seen.
He has been playing football since he was 14 years of age but this is the first
time he has had a chance to prove his ability in big-time competition.
This means he is competing with and against far more experienced players
and undoubtedly he has much to learn. He may make many mistakes in his
fans will love him.
